Two results overturned the plan. First, the v3 idea of reserving a quarter of the slots for era covers is negative value: a cover slot is worth 0.10 expected hits against 0.32 for a rotation slot, because the cover pool is huge and flat — covers get played, but no individual cover is a good bet. Second, v4's distinctiveness term looked strong until it was tested properly; fitted on one real night and scored on the other it managed 12/40, worse than just ranking by play count. Both were cut.

What survived is duller and works better. Both real setlists drew from the middle of the theme year's ranking — median rank 29 of 101 on 7/22 and 41 of 83 on 7/24, with only 7 of ~25 songs coming from the top 20 either night. Sweeping the head/mid-depth split across all 21 settings, every one from 0 to 17 head picks beat a straight top-20. So tonight's slate votes across the whole family rather than trusting the single best split, which on two shows is noise. Held out, that construction scores 19/40 — and 11/20 on 7/22, the best any method has managed on that night. Expected tonight: 9.3 of 20, averaged over three different assumptions about how a setlist gets built.

The honest caveat: three scored shows is a small sample, the depth kernel failed its own cross-validation, and the pool shrinks every night, so accuracy should rise through the run for reasons that have nothing to do with the model getting smarter.
