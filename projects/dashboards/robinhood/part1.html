The part most people get wrong, so read it twice. Selling stock does not increase your margin equity. $88,081 of stock less $59,389 of debt is $28,693 of equity, and it is still $28,693 after you sell $8,463 — the money just moves from one column to the other. What selling actually buys you is a smaller book, so the same dollar cushion absorbs a bigger percentage move (your floor-to-book distance goes from 4.2% to 4.6%), less interest, and fewer things that can go wrong at once. The only two things that raise margin equity in dollars are a cash deposit and the market going up. If what you want is real PDT headroom, deposit — do not sell.

The market acting on you — back-cast index, Apr 1 to Jul 27

Read this carefully, because it is not your account value. This takes the share counts you hold today in the fifteen largest positions and values them backwards on split-adjusted daily closes. It answers one question and only one: what has the market done to the shares I currently own? It deliberately ignores every deposit, withdrawal, buy and sell, which is exactly what makes it useful — nothing in this line is you. It is also why it cannot be reconciled against your statement. Two honest caveats: it covers 14 names until Jun 26 and 15 after, because one of them has barely twenty sessions of history, so the small step there is coverage and not performance; and it excludes crypto, cash and margin debt entirely, so it is an index of your stock picks, not of your net worth.

What it says. The shares you own peaked at $100,287 on May 29 and are worth $65,118 now — -35.1% off the high in 38 sessions, and only -4.2% away from where the same shares started on April 1. The whole year, on this basis, is one clean run-up from early May into late May and one long unbroken bleed since. The worst five-session stretch was -18.0% (May 29 → Jun 5); the best was +21.6% (May 7 → May 14). The asymmetry is the finding: your up-moves came in a burst and your down-move came as a grind, which is the exact pattern that makes a portfolio feel survivable while it is halving.

When you see a strategy on this dashboard that looks like it works, check first whether its profits land in September and October 2025. Usually they do.

How to use this going forward. The single most valuable habit you can build is writing down why before you click, because the reason is the only thing you can score later — the outcome tells you almost nothing over a handful of trades. A good decision that loses money is still a good decision; 128 trades is nowhere near enough to distinguish the two statistically, which is precisely why process has to be judged on process. Three things I will keep tracking here: whether the yield-ETF exits look right six months out, whether the BITO limit fills or you end up chasing it down, and whether the realised-P&L bars ever produce a second cluster that is not September 2025.
