Read the last column first. At a 30% house maintenance requirement you have $17,784 of room today — down from $20,681 yesterday, because the debit went up. None of the single-name gaps above breach it, and even a broad −8% day leaves roughly $10,600; the point is the direction, not any one line. Robinhood’s published maintenance requirement is 25% for most equities, but house requirements are raised at the broker’s discretion on concentrated, volatile or recently-halted names — which describes a large share of this book — and they are raised precisely when volatility spikes, which is to say the week of an earnings gap. The 25% column is the legal floor; the 30% column is the realistic one. Neither triggers on paper losses alone: a call is met by depositing cash or by the broker selling your positions for you, at its timing rather than yours.

The recommendation, plainly: pause the re-levering, and do not add margin into the NVDA print. You are paying about $2,409 a year in interest on a $50,182 debit, and today you added the most leveraged instrument in the book — a 3× semis ETF — on borrowed money, six days before the account’s largest scheduled event. That is the exact trade this page has argued against for three weeks, and the arithmetic has not changed. If you want a new position, sell something you like less; do not borrow another dollar with the cushion already narrower than yesterday. The winners are still there to fund it: RKLB ($14,582, +76%), NOW ($10,379, +43%), NVDA ($9,765, +28%), LUNR ($3,588, +91%), PL ($2,771, +394%), HOOD ($1,902, +131%) — and trimming into that list does double duty on the names with dates. Raising, say, $6,000 there does not lift your margin equity by a cent — the shares become cash, the cash pays the debit, and $39,291 stays $39,291 — but it buys distance from a call: the debit falls toward $44,000, the 25% cushion widens back past 28%, and you stop paying interest on money you did not need to borrow. NVDA is on that winner list, so trimming it cuts the debit and shrinks the Aug 26 tail at once. Standing still is defensible — the floor is $14,291 away and no single print can reach it. Adding leverage into the print is not. You did a little of the wrong one today; the fix is to stop, not to compound it.
