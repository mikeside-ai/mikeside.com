The formula, in full. Every position starts at 65. Unrealized return moves it first: losses subtract 0.35 points per percent down but stop at −25, gains add 0.4 per percent up capped at +40. The cap is deliberate and it is the most important design choice here — being down a lot is not, on its own, enough to fail. What fails a position is the wrapper: 30 points off a leveraged or yield-overlay ETF for structural decay, 35 off a warrant, 22 off anything under a dollar and 10 off anything under three, and 12 off any position worth less than $150 because it cannot change your outcome. Concentration is penalised lightly — 6 points off a name above 12% of the book, 3 above 8%, 3 off anything inside a theme worth more than a quarter of the portfolio — because concentration is a portfolio problem the rest of this page already handles, not a defect in the position itself. A move worse than −5% today costs 4; better than +3% adds 3; a profitable position over $2,000 adds 4. Score is clamped to 0–100 and banded A 78+, B 64, C 48, D 36, F below. The practical consequence: an ordinary stock you are simply losing money on bottoms out around a D, while an F almost always means the instrument is broken — a decaying wrapper, an out-of-the-money warrant, a sub-dollar delisting candidate, or a lot too small to matter. What it deliberately does not know: anything about the business. It has no revenue, no balance sheet, no valuation and no view on whether the thesis is right. A great company you bought badly grades poorly here, and it should — the grade is about the position, not the company. Do not treat a letter as a buy or sell instruction.
