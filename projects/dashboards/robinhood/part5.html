Staking — my answer is no, and here's the arithmetic

Robinhood supports staking on SOL, ETH and ADA only, and takes a commission of 25% of the gross reward rate (a partner validator fee plus Robinhood's cut). The rate shown in the app is already net of that. Here is what you'd actually earn against what your margin costs you at the 4.8% tier:

Straight read: you are being offered 2–5% on assets you already own while paying 4.8% to borrow. Even SOL — the only one that even reaches break-even — loses once you account for tax: staking rewards are ordinary income at receipt, so a 5% net yield is more like 3–3.7% in your pocket, against a 4.8% cost. Meanwhile margin interest is only deductible if you itemize and have net investment income to offset it. You'd be locking up liquidity, accepting slashing and validator risk, and keeping 100% of the price volatility — to earn a negative spread. Selling some crypto and paying down margin is a guaranteed, tax-free 4.8% return. Staking is a worse, riskier, less liquid version of the same trade. The only version of staking that makes sense for you is after the margin is gone.

4. The tax asymmetry worth knowing Ask your CPA

Under current US treatment, direct crypto is property, not a security, so the 30-day wash-sale rule does not apply to it the way it does to BITO, MSTY and the miners. If a coin is underwater you can harvest the loss and buy it back immediately, keeping your exposure while banking the deduction. On the equities, you must stay out 30 days to keep the loss.

Congress has repeatedly proposed closing this gap, and it could change with little warning. Reward income from staking is separately taxable as ordinary income at receipt — even if you never sell the coin, which catches people out.

Straight read: potentially the most valuable line on this page, and the one I'm least qualified to sign off on. This genuinely needs a CPA before you act — the mechanics are simple, the rules are jurisdiction- and year-specific, and I can't see your basis or your realized gains for the year.

The counter-intuitive one: sell losers before they turn long-term

Everyone knows to hold winners past a year for the lower rate. The mirror image is less obvious and more useful to you right now: a short-term loss is worth more than a long-term loss, because it offsets short-term gains first — and short-term gains are taxed at your ordinary rate rather than 15%. Several of your losing positions have short-term lots about to cross the one-year line, at which point their tax value quietly drops by roughly a third. Nearest first: GPRO in 3 days, OPENW in 45 days, LCID in 99 days. If you were going to cut them anyway, cutting them on the near side of that date is free money.

Wash sales — one live flag

Selling at a loss and buying the same or a “substantially identical” security within 30 days either side disallows the loss and rolls the basis forward. The trap is the buy that already happened: nothing in your book was purchased inside the last 30 days at a loss. Your other recent purchases are all outside the window. And the asymmetry worth repeating: direct crypto is treated as property rather than a security, so the rule does not bite it the same way — you can harvest a coin loss and re-buy immediately. Congress has proposed closing that gap more than once, so do not build a permanent plan around it.
