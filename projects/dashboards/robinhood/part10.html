Week trading — 56 names with real weekly swing, and two strategies run over all of them

A week-long holding period is a genuinely better fit for this account than a day-long one. It sidesteps the pattern-day-trader rule entirely, it does not require you to sit at a screen, and the moves are large enough that the spread stops being the dominant cost. So this got a full twelve months of daily data rather than the three months the day-trade tab had to work with. The names below are screened for exactly what you described — things that swing up and down week to week — and the two strategies are the two honest ways to trade that: buy the dip, or buy the breakout.

The finding that matters more than any other number on this page. Strategy K appears to return +912% summed over 471 trades, which looks like the answer to everything. It is not. Its median trade is -1.78% — more than half of them lose — it wins only 36.5% of the time, and ten trades supply 88% of the entire profit. Strip out its best twenty-five and expectancy flips to -1.02% per trade. Now look at when those trades happened: September and October 2025, in the quantum and nuclear melt-up, led by RGTI, QBTS, IONQ and OKLO. That is the same four-week window that produced 91% of your own realised profit for the year. Your trailing-year +$14,958 and this backtest's +912% are not two pieces of evidence. They are one event wearing two costumes. If you trade K expecting 1.94% a week you are betting that autumn 2025 happens again on schedule — and everything between the top 25 trades and today says the ordinary weeks lose money.

What the two strategies did — 2025-07-25 → 2026-07-24

Strategy M — buy the dip

Enter when the close falls more than 1.5 ATRs below the 20-day average; target the average, stop 2 ATRs lower, time out after 10 sessions. 426 trades, 45.8% winners, average winner +9.6% against an average loser of -8.8%, expectancy -0.47% net, profit factor 0.90, summed -199.8%, average hold 7.5 days. Exits split 41% at target, 18% stopped, 41% timed out. This one fails cleanly and there is nothing to salvage: removing its twenty-five best trades leaves expectancy at -1.96%, meaning the loss is spread across the whole book of trades, not caused by a few disasters. Buying weakness in high-volatility names is not a strategy, it is a description of how these accounts get built.

Strategy K — buy the breakout

Enter on a 20-day high, exit when the close drops below the 10-day average, with a 2 ATR stop. 471 trades, 36.5% winners, average winner +14.2% against -5.2%, expectancy 1.94% net, profit factor 1.58, average hold 7.2 days. The asymmetry is the whole mechanism — winners run nearly three times as far as losers fall, which is exactly what a trend rule is supposed to produce. But note the exit split: 91.1% of exits came from the moving-average rule and only 0.2% from the stop, so the 2 ATR stop is decorative. In a fast gap-down the moving average has not updated yet and the stop is too far away to help. The downside is unmanaged by design. Best per-symbol: RGTI +20.7% (n=5), INTC +16.5% (n=8), CLSK +13.3% (n=7), SOXL +12.9% (n=11), IREN +11.1% (n=9). Worst: LCID -5.5% (n=4), SOFI -5.0% (n=10), RIVN -4.2% (n=11), UVXY -4.1% (n=6), COIN -4.0% (n=8) — and with a median of about eight trades per symbol, both lists are mostly noise.

Reading the table. Weekly swing is the average high-to-low range within a calendar week as a percentage of price, with the standard deviation underneath — a big swing with a big deviation means the volatility itself is unstable, which is worse than it sounds because it makes position sizing guesswork. Reversals count how many times in the last year the weekly direction flipped; above about 22 the name genuinely oscillates rather than trends, which is what you were asking for. “vs 20d” and “vs 50d” are distance from those moving averages in percent, and dollar volume is in millions per day — anything under about $20M is thin enough that a real position moves the price against you on the way out. Window is 2025-07-25 → 2026-07-24, daily bars, regular hours.

The caveats, exactly as the analysis returned them

  1. STRATEGY M LOSES MONEY: -0.47%/trade net, PF 0.90, -199.8% summed over 426 trades; it is unprofitable gross AND net and the loss is broad-based, not outlier-driven (expectancy still -1.96% after removing its 25 best trades).
  2. STRATEGY K's +1.94%/trade net is NOT a reliable edge: median trade is -1.88%, only 36.5% win, and 10 trades supply 88% of all profit (top 25 supply 150%). Excluding the top 25 trades expectancy is -1.02%/trade. The P&L is a lottery-ticket right tail concentrated in the Sep-Oct 2025 quantum/nuclear melt-up (RGTI +103% single trade, QBTS/IONQ/OKLO similar) - a one-off regime, not a repeatable process.
  3. K's 2xATR stop is nearly inert (0.2% of exits) - the MA10 rule always fires first, so downside is unmanaged by the stop as designed.
  4. Single 12-month sample, one bull-skewed regime, no walk-forward, no out-of-sample split, survivorship-biased universe (hand-picked 2025-26 momentum names). n per symbol is small (median ~8 trades), so per-symbol rankings and top12/bottom12 are mostly noise - do not size positions off them.
  5. Last bar 2026-07-24 close is not an official settled close. X excluded: inactive/delisted instrument, API 400.
So what would I actually do here? Watch it for a quarter before risking a dollar, and watch the right thing. The question is not “does K make money” — over this sample it did, and that tells you almost nothing. The question is whether K makes money in a window that excludes September and October 2025. That is a test you can run for free by logging what the signals say each week and marking them to market, and it will take about ten minutes a week. If the ordinary weeks come out positive, you have found something worth a small amount of capital. If they come out negative — which is what the top-25 exclusion arithmetic implies — then you have saved yourself from funding a strategy whose entire history is one lucky autumn. Either way you will know, which you do not today. And if you do eventually trade it: the position size has to assume the median trade, not the average one. The median K trade loses 1.78%. Sizing off the +1.94% average is how a strategy with a real edge still blows up an account.
