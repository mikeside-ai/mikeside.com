The market acting on you — back-cast index, Apr 1 to Aug 11

Read this carefully, because it is not your account value. This takes the share counts you hold today in the fourteen largest positions with a usable price history and values them backwards on split-adjusted daily closes. It answers one question and only one: what has the market done to the shares I currently own? It deliberately ignores every deposit, withdrawal, buy and sell, which is exactly what makes it useful — nothing in this line is you. It is also why it cannot be reconciled against your statement. Three honest caveats: it covers 13 names until Jun 26 and 14 after, because HONA has only listed since late June, so the small step there is coverage and not performance; XOM is left out entirely even though it is a top-fifteen position, because the price feed only carries real bars for it back to July and a flat synthetic line would have quietly damped every move in this chart; and it excludes crypto, cash and margin debt, so it is an index of your stock picks, not of your net worth. Aug 4 is priced off live closing prints rather than the daily bar, which had not settled when this was built.

What it says, and what changed. The shares you own peaked at $87,413 on May 29 and are worth $66,092 now — still -24.4% off the high 50 sessions later — but they are now 16.5% above where the same shares started on April 1, and +24.2% above the Jul 29 low. Until last week this line read as one clean run-up into late May and one long grind down; that description is no longer true. The single session of +0.2% on Aug 4, driven overwhelmingly by PLTR's print, is the largest one-day move in the series and it undid roughly six weeks of the bleed in a day. The worst five-session stretch was -18.9% (May 29 → Jun 5); the best was +25.4% (May 7 → May 14). Do not over-learn from this. The same concentration that produced -0.8% in two sessions is what produced the drawdown in the first place, and it is still there — four names are more than half the book. A portfolio that can gain +0.2% on one earnings report can lose it on the next one.

The uncomfortable version. Your trailing-year realised P&L is +$15,513 across 140 trades and it looks like a track record. It is not one. 88% of it came from four weeks — Sep 27, Oct 4, Sep 20, Sep 6 — and those four weeks are the September–October 2025 quantum and nuclear melt-up. Take them out and the remaining 18 active weeks of trading produced about $1,845 between them, which is roughly what the margin interest on this account costs in 10 months. You do not have a trading edge; you had a regime. That matters far more than it sounds, because everything else on this page — the day-trade backtests, the swing backtests, the momentum strategy that appears to return +912% — keeps rediscovering the same four weeks wearing different costumes. When you see a strategy on this dashboard that looks like it works, check first whether its profits land in September and October 2025. Usually they do.

One of those fills was not a decision you made this week. The BP sale was a $45.00 GTC limit order you placed on May 27, sixty-five days earlier, and it filled by itself at 1:31pm on Jul 31 when the stock finally traded up to your price. I checked the rest of the book: there is exactly one other working order, the NOW sell below, and no other old limits waiting to surprise you.

“Since fill” scores the timing, not the decision. A sell that looks good because the price fell afterwards was still the right sell for the reason you made it, and a sell that looks bad because the price bounced is not evidence you should have held — over ten tickets this column is almost entirely noise. The column that scores the decision is the debit, and the debit went down $7,537. Positions shown as closed no longer appear in the holdings feed.

Straight version: you are back above the floor, and the market did most of the lifting. Margin equity went from $662 short of the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum to $17,954 above it. It is worth being precise about where that came from, because it is not where it feels like it came from. Cash-neutral trades cannot move margin equity — selling a share just turns it into cash that pays down the debit, leaving equity exactly where it was. Strip out every fill and the retained book still rose about $16,484 over the two sessions on price alone. That is the entire gain. The selling did something different and also valuable: it bought distance from a maintenance call. A 25% call now needs securities to fall to $64,480, which is 29.4% below here, against roughly 7% a week ago. The part that still needs your attention: the floor cushion is $17,954 on a $91,314 book, so a 19.7% decline puts you straight back under it — and PLTR reports Monday. See the This week tab.

How to use this going forward. The single most valuable habit you can build is writing down why before you click, because the reason is the only thing you can score later — the outcome tells you almost nothing over a handful of trades. A good decision that loses money is still a good decision; 140 trades is nowhere near enough to distinguish the two statistically, which is precisely why process has to be judged on process. Three things I will keep tracking here: whether the yield-ETF exits look right six months out — the block is now fully closed, MSTY included — whether the PANW and RVII buys turn out to have been worth borrowing for, and whether the realised-P&L bars ever produce a second cluster that is not September 2025.
