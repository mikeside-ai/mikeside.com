The market acting on you — back-cast index, Apr 1 to Jul 31

Read this carefully, because it is not your account value. This takes the share counts you hold today in the fifteen largest positions and values them backwards on split-adjusted daily closes. It answers one question and only one: what has the market done to the shares I currently own? It deliberately ignores every deposit, withdrawal, buy and sell, which is exactly what makes it useful — nothing in this line is you. It is also why it cannot be reconciled against your statement. Two honest caveats: it covers 14 names until Jun 26 and 15 after, because one of them has barely twenty sessions of history, so the small step there is coverage and not performance; and it excludes crypto, cash and margin debt entirely, so it is an index of your stock picks, not of your net worth.

What it says. The shares you own peaked at $96,314 on May 29 and are worth $62,673 now — -34.9% off the high in 43 sessions, and only -2.6% away from where the same shares started on April 1. The whole year, on this basis, is one clean run-up from early May into late May and one long unbroken bleed since. The worst five-session stretch was -18.0% (May 29 → Jun 5); the best was +22.8% (May 7 → May 14). The asymmetry is the finding: your up-moves came in a burst and your down-move came as a grind, which is the exact pattern that makes a portfolio feel survivable while it is halving.

The uncomfortable version. Your trailing-year realised P&L is +$15,342 across 140 trades and it looks like a track record. It is not one. 89% of it came from four weeks — Sep 27, Oct 4, Sep 20, Sep 6 — and those four weeks are the September–October 2025 quantum and nuclear melt-up. Take them out and the remaining 18 active weeks of trading produced about $1,674 between them, which is roughly what the margin interest on this account costs in 8 months. You do not have a trading edge; you had a regime. That matters far more than it sounds, because everything else on this page — the day-trade backtests, the swing backtests, the momentum strategy that appears to return +912% — keeps rediscovering the same four weeks wearing different costumes. When you see a strategy on this dashboard that looks like it works, check first whether its profits land in September and October 2025. Usually they do.

“Since fill” scores the timing, not the decision. A sell that looks good because the price fell afterwards was still the right sell for the reason you made it, and a sell that looks bad because the price bounced is not evidence you should have held — over ten tickets this column is almost entirely noise. The column that scores the decision is the debit, and the debit went down $4,263. Positions shown as closed no longer appear in the holdings feed.

Straight version: you are back above the floor, and the market did most of the lifting. Margin equity went from $662 short of the $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum to $2,187 above it. It is worth being precise about where that came from, because it is not where it feels like it came from. Cash-neutral trades cannot move margin equity — selling a share just turns it into cash that pays down the debit, leaving equity exactly where it was. Strip out every fill and the retained book still rose about $2,849 over the two sessions on price alone. That is the entire gain. The selling did something different and also valuable: it bought distance from a maintenance call. A 25% call now needs securities to fall to $68,845, which is 12.7% below here, against roughly 7% a week ago. The part that still needs your attention: the floor cushion is $2,187 on a $78,821 book, so a 2.8% decline puts you straight back under it — and PLTR reports Monday. See the This week tab.

How to use this going forward. The single most valuable habit you can build is writing down why before you click, because the reason is the only thing you can score later — the outcome tells you almost nothing over a handful of trades. A good decision that loses money is still a good decision; 140 trades is nowhere near enough to distinguish the two statistically, which is precisely why process has to be judged on process. Three things I will keep tracking here: whether the yield-ETF exits look right six months out, whether the BITO limit fills or you end up chasing it down, and whether the realised-P&L bars ever produce a second cluster that is not September 2025.
