Straight read. Your account equity is 30.3% right now — above the 25% FINRA maintenance floor, below the 50% Reg T initial requirement. That is exactly why your buying power is $6,103.21: you are not in trouble, but you have no capacity left either. At a flat 25% requirement a 7.1% market drop triggers a maintenance call. At 30% it is 0.5%. At 35% you are already there.

Which of those is real depends on Robinhood’s house requirement for your specific holdings, and this dashboard cannot see it. What it can see is the composition: leveraged and yield-overlay ETFs, several sub-$5 names and a handful under $1. Those are precisely the securities brokers surcharge above the floor, often to 50–100%. Assume you are nearer the 30% line than the 25% line until Robinhood tells you otherwise.

The structural point most people miss: your $13,484 of crypto is not collateral for an equity margin call. Robinhood holds coins at a separate entity from the brokerage. It cushions your net worth and does nothing for your maintenance math. On the numbers above, a 39% broad drawdown takes your entire net account to zero — not because the stocks go to zero, but because the debit does not shrink when the assets do.

Simplifications, stated plainly: this treats every holding as falling by the same percentage, which understates the damage because your book is higher-beta than the market; it applies one blended maintenance rate rather than Robinhood’s per-security schedule; it assumes the debit is static, ignoring accrued interest; and it ignores the fact that a real forced liquidation happens at whatever price the market offers that morning, not at the close. This is a risk-sizing tool, not a prediction, and it is not investment advice.
