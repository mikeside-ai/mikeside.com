Family trip · Walton County, Florida

Beach week at Seagrove

Six days on 30A with a six-year-old, a two-year-old and a nine-month-old. Beach early, nap hard, eat at five. Everything below is built around that rhythm.

Read this before anything else

Labor Day is Monday, September 7. You are arriving into the busiest weekend 30A sees after July. Friday through Monday means full beach-access lots by 9am, hour waits at every restaurant, and heavy northbound traffic on US-331 on Monday. Tuesday the 8th, it empties out like someone pulled a drain plug. The schedule below leans local and early through the weekend and saves the driving for Sunday and Tuesday.

Text FLAG to 31279 before you leave. That's the South Walton Fire District beach flag alert. Double red means the Gulf is legally closed and the fine is $500. Purple means jellyfish. On a red-flag day with these three, knee deep really is too deep.

Load-in

Thursday's drive

I-65 south to Montgomery, then US-331 all the way down through Luverne, Opp and Florala to Freeport, then east on 30A. It's the standard Birmingham-to-30A route and most of the back half is two-lane. Leaving at six buys you the empty half of US-331 and puts you at the house near check-in.

The pool is the actual risk on this trip

Drowning is the leading cause of death for children ages one to four, and vacation rentals are where it happens — unfamiliar layout, unfenced pool, sliding doors that don't latch, and four adults each assuming another one is watching. Within ten minutes of walking in the door: check whether the pool has a working gate and self-latching lock, and check every ground-floor door for a chime or alarm. If there's no barrier between Slavik and open water, one adult is the designated water watcher at all times, phone down, and you hand that job off out loud. Everything else on this page is optional. That isn't.

The week

Day by day

One anchor per day, nothing more. Beach from 8 to 11 before the heat, the crowds and the parking; house from noon to three for lunch and naps; something low-effort in the late afternoon; dinner at five because every good place on 30A is a 90-minute wait by 6:30. Highs are upper 80s all week with afternoon storm chances, so mornings are not just easier, they're drier.

Two things to check before you drive

Heights will decide this day. Race City's three big tracks — Gran Prix, PCB 500 and the Speed Track — all require 54 inches. A six-year-old is usually around 45, so plan on Cash not driving those. The kiddie track, Lil' Speedy, is 42 inches and he should clear it comfortably. Slavik at two clears nothing. The Speed Track lists a 400 lb double-kart capacity, so an adult may be able to take Cash as a passenger, but that is not stated anywhere on their site. Ask at the ticket window first, and measure Cash at the house before you leave so nobody finds out in the parking lot.

Call ahead. Both places have question marks. Race City's own hours page still carries a stale "closed for the winter, reopening February 2026" banner and lists Monday through Wednesday as closed, with Sunday opening at noon. That is why this is a Sunday and not Labor Day Monday. Confirm it at racecitypcb.com/hours or by phone the night before. Gulf World Marine Park, the dolphin park, is the obvious third stop, but its booking page says the habitat is "in maintenance" — call (850) 234-5271 before building any part of the day around it. Coconut Creek is the reliable one: 9:00a to 11:30p daily, (850) 234-2625.

Rained out or everyone melts down? Swap the whole afternoon for Eden Gardens State Park, 181 Eden Gardens Rd, 15 minutes north on 395. Moss-draped live oaks, an antebellum house, picnic tables, a dock on Tucker Bayou, and deep shade the whole way, which is the version of this day that suits Snejana best. Small honor-system fee, bring singles.

Set II

When it rains or the flags go red

There's a 35–45% storm chance most afternoons this week, and a red or double-red flag day is entirely possible in September. Have these in your pocket so nobody has to invent a plan with three tired kids in a rental house.

Gulfarium Marine Adventure Park

Fort Walton Beach · 45 min west · 9:00–5:30 daily

The best bad-weather pick for this exact age spread. Dolphin and sea lion shows on a schedule, touch tanks, mostly covered. Cash gets shows, Slavik gets animals, Snejana rides. Book any animal encounter online ahead — they sell out.

The Big Chill 30A

Watersound · 8 min east · 7:00a–10:00p

Covered seating, arcade, ice cream, movie on the screen. Costs nothing to show up and works for two hours on short notice.

Coastal Branch Library

437 Greenway Trail · 20 min · closed Sundays

Free, air conditioned, genuinely good children's section. Underrated when you need ninety quiet minutes and everyone's had enough of each other.

Baytowne Wharf, Sandestin

Miramar Beach · 30 min west

Village square with a playground, candy shops and Blast Arcade nearby. The zipline and ropes course are Cash-only and probably a next-trip thing.

Grayton Beach State Park

357 Main Park Rd · 12 min west

Backup beach if Eastern Lake is packed. Big lot, real restrooms, kayak rentals on Western Lake — the lake side is calm and shallow when the Gulf isn't.

Skip: Big Kahuna's

Destin · 35 min · reduced Sept hours, closed Tuesdays

Full-price admission for a three-year-old and a nine-month-old who can ride almost nothing, plus paid parking. Not worth it with this crew.

Three different trips

What each of them actually needs

Cash

6 years

  • He needs one thing per day that's his: crab hunting, the boogie board, the shell hunt, the arcade.
  • Give him a job. Wagon captain, sunscreen checker, shell counter. It cuts the whining in half.
  • He's the only one who can handle a full afternoon out. Split up sometimes — one adult and Cash, one adult and the little two.
  • Watch him hardest in the water. Six-year-olds overestimate themselves and 30A has a double sandbar that builds real rip currents.

Slavik

2, almost 3

  • He's fast, he has no fear of water, and he can't swim. He wears the vest anywhere near the pool, full stop.
  • Two hours is his beach ceiling. Leave before he tells you he's done.
  • The nap is not optional. Every bad evening this week traces back to a skipped nap.
  • He'll eat sand. Everyone eats sand at two. Keep water close and don't make it a fight.

Snejana

9 months

  • Shade, always. Tent up before she comes out of the carrier.
  • Sunscreen is fine at her age, but shade and fabric do most of the work.
  • The warm shallow lake outfall at Eastern Lake will be her favorite place on earth.
  • She overheats faster than anyone will notice. Offer fluids constantly and pull her inside by 10:30.
  • Protect her sleep space hardest — that's what determines whether the adults enjoy this trip.
