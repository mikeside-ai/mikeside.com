Show #94 in progress
How the picks are made, and what tonight is teaching model v7 · live

They are playing a Tweezerfest. Tweezer has appeared five times in set two, wrapping Down with Disease, Sample in a Jar, Lifeboy, Julius, Catapult and Cavern. That is a direct restaging of 6/18/94 — the most famous structural gag of the theme year.

The note written into the Tweezer pick this afternoon read “1994 is its defining year — the Bomb Factory version, Tweezerfest.” That was meant as colour. It turned out to be literal, which makes hedging Tweezer down from 8 points to 5 the clearest scoring error of the night.

The structural miss: Halloween costume songs

Tonight opened with Back in the U.S.S.R., a 485-show bustout last played in 2013 — a White Album track, and Phish played the White Album in full as their 1994 Halloween musical costume. Highway to Hell followed at a 659-show gap.

A play-count model structurally cannot find these songs. Halloween-costume material was played once or twice in the theme year, so it sits at the bottom of any frequency ranking while being among the most era-defining music the band has. Top rule for v8: the theme year's Halloween album gets its own reserved bucket. Highway to Hell is the same failure from the other side — 15 plays in 1994, ranked 32nd, just outside the cut.

The bustout structure held, even though the pick failed

Three deep bustouts tonight: Back in the U.S.S.R. (485), Highway to Hell (659), Catapult (223). Nights #92 and #93 each produced 3–4 songs past a 300-show gap, and tonight lands squarely inside that. The structural finding is confirmed; the naming of it remains impossible. Bustout should be reframed as a predicted count, or a category, not a title.

Longest jam: the narrative beat the data

The call was You Enjoy Myself on jamchart entries, 27 to Tweezer's 23. It is Tweezer, by a distance. The quantitative signal pointed one way, the era-narrative pointed the other, and the narrative won — the same lesson the Tweezer hedge taught.

Openers may simply be unwinnable

Backtested against nights 1 and 2, the opener model ranked the actual opener 14th and 3rd, and no variant improved it. Tonight it was a 485-show bustout. Three data points, no model in reach: this category should be reframed or dropped rather than tuned.

Dormancy: still do not filter on it

Both earlier nights ran a median gap of 10–11 shows with 3–4 songs past a 300-show gap, and tonight fits exactly — a current-rotation core plus three vault moments. A gap penalty would have thrown away the show's most memorable material, and no gap-based model could have picked it either.

Why this page is not auto-polling

Live results are baked in and re-published rather than fetched in the browser. Auto-polling would require the phish.net API key in the source of a public repository. The clean path is the existing setlist bot's phishpicks ingest endpoint, which already receives one JSON record per song and needs no key on the read side.
