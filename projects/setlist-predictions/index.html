Phish @ MSG — Setlist Predictions

5 NIGHTS · #92–96

Five-night Madison Square Garden run celebrating Phish's 92nd through 96th shows at the Garden — each night themed to a year, 1992 → 1996. Twenty predicted songs per night, each with the reason it was picked; check them off as they're played. Everything is editable — click a song to rename it, ✕ to remove, + add for your own. The number beside each pick is how many times it was played in its theme year. Predictions never reuse a song played earlier in the run — Phish doesn't repeat across a residency, and the two played nights share exactly zero songs.

How the picks for nights 3–5 are made model v7 · measured, not estimated

This model got simpler because the data said it should. Every earlier version — including the one on this page a few hours ago — was tuned on intuition about what a themed night “feels like.” With the phish.net API wired in, the play counts are now measured: 1994 alone is 147 shows, 3,084 song rows, 340 distinct songs.

The measurement killed both the old model and its replacement. What survives is the dullest possible rule, and it beats everything else tested.

The error that ran through every previous version

v5 concluded that setlists draw from the middle of a year's frequency ranking, and built its picks around a “20–60% depth band.” That was an artifact of ranking each song within the full theme-year pool instead of the eligible pool — the one left after striking everything already played earlier in the run.

A correction to this page's own earlier update

Earlier today this model was revised to reserve three slots for “era-signature covers,” reasoning that night 2 played six covers so v5 was wrong to exclude them. The play counts say v5 was right and that revision was wrong. Covers get played, but no individual cover is a good standalone bet — the pool is enormous and flat, exactly as v5 argued. Amazing Grace is on tonight's slate purely because it was played 31 times in 1994, which is how a cover should earn a slot: on frequency, not a reserved quota. Loving Cup, Frankenstein and the Purple Rain vacuum solo were cut, as was Wolfman's Brother — promoted this afternoon because Hoist is the 1994 record, which is true and irrelevant, since it was barely played live that year. Album year is not play frequency.

The model, in full

1. Pull every song played in the theme year from phish.net; count plays. 2. Strike every song already played earlier in this run. 3. Take the top 20. That's it — no depth band, no cover quota, no archetype buckets, no simulation. The run no-repeat rule in step 2 is the only real insight here, and it's free: 50 songs are off the board for tonight.

What writing the reasons exposed — the next thing to fix

Attaching a sourced rationale to all 60 picks surfaced a blind spot. v7 ranks on theme-year plays and completely ignores whether a song is anywhere near current rotation. Tonight is mostly fine — 16 of 20 picks have been played within the last dozen shows — but Amazing Grace carries a 900-show gap. Nights 4 and 5 are far more exposed: Acoustic Army hasn't appeared in 1,096 shows, Suspicious Minds 1,019, Johnny B. Goode 926, I'm Blue I'm Lonesome 791.

This is deliberately not fixed yet, because the right answer is genuinely unknown. A year-themed night is exactly the occasion a band reaches for dormant era material — night 2 proved it, opening the vault for Big Ball Jam at a 1,170-show gap and the first Kung since 2019. So a dormancy penalty might improve the model or might destroy the thing that makes themed nights predictable at all. Tonight's result is the first real test: if Amazing Grace lands, dormancy is noise; if the deep cuts miss across nights 3–5, v8 gets a gap term.

The honest caveat

One scored blind night is the entire evidentiary base, and v7's measured margin on it is 1.2 songs out of 20 — well inside noise. The strongest available claim is that v7 is simpler than what it replaced, is fitted to nothing, and would have beaten the published slate on the only night both could be scored against. Expect scores to drift up regardless as the eligible pool shrinks, whether or not the model is any good.

Set placement is cosmetic. The model predicts which twenty songs, not the running order, and scoring only counts whether a song was played at all.
