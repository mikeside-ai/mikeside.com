The costume-album rule

Phish's Halloween “musical costume” tradition — covering an entire album — ran from 1994 until the band started writing their own costumes with Wingsuit/Fuego. The rule fires for exactly the years that have one and stays silent for the years that don't:

Which costume song, though? Two sub-rules and only one data point to separate them. The repertoire pick is the costume song that entered rotation — for Quadrophenia that is overwhelmingly Drowned (48 all-time plays; the next-best, Sea and Sand, has 4). The album-opener pick is the tell from #94: they used track one of the White Album to open the show. Quadrophenia opens I Am the Sea → The Real Me.

On #94 they chose the deep album-opener (U.S.S.R., 485-show gap) over the repertoire song (While My Guitar Gently Weeps, 38 all-time). v8 carries both — Drowned on the slate and ladder, The Real Me as the opener call.

v8 in full

Slate, 20 songs, two tiers. Tier A — 17 slots: v7 unchanged, theme-year play count minus everything burned in the run, top down. Tier B — 3 reserved slots: costume album · Fish/novelty · the most era-locked song available, meaning the highest share of its all-time plays falling inside the theme year.

Applied retroactively to #94, Tier B swaps the three weakest Tier A picks — Amazing Grace, Nellie Kane and Wilson, all of which missed — for Back in the U.S.S.R. and Purple Rain: 12/20 instead of 10/20. That is an in-sample result fitted to the night it explains, so Monday is the real test.

Ladder is unchanged in method: 10 picks, strictly probability-ranked, because Σ points × P is maximised by pairing biggest points with biggest probability. Raw play rate stays calibrated — it predicted 8.77 against 9 actual on #92 and 8.41 against 7 on #93.

Called shots, reframed after 0-for-4

Opener is now the costume track-one pick rather than the frequency leader. Frequency backtested at rank 14 and rank 3 on the first two nights and missed entirely on the third. Encore is now the Fish/novelty slot — Purple Rain on #94, and the a cappella Sweet Adeline on #93. Longest jam follows era-narrative over jamchart count.

Bustout becomes a count, not a name. The three nights produced 3, 4 and 3 songs past a 300-show gap. The count is reliably 3–4; the identity is unreachable, since no individual candidate clears 10%. Predicting the number is a bet that can actually be won.

What has not changed

Run no-repeat — now 66 songs burned — remains the single most valuable rule and it is free. Do not filter on dormancy: #94 ran a current-rotation core plus three deep bustouts, exactly like #92 and #93. A gap penalty would have discarded the night's most memorable material.

Still rejected: mid-depth sampling, blanket cover quotas, Secret-Language weighting (collinear with play count), and skipping the obvious — every variant scored worse on the bulk slate.
