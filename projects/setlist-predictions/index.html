Live verdict so far: the ladder is working and the called shots are not. The miss that matters: Halloween costume songs

Tonight opened with Back in the U.S.S.R., a 485-show bustout last played in 2013. It is a White Album track — and Phish played the White Album in full as their 1994 Halloween musical costume. Highway to Hell followed in set two at a 659-show gap.

A play-count model structurally cannot find these songs. Halloween-costume material was played once or twice in the theme year, so it sits at the very bottom of any frequency ranking while being among the most era-defining music the band has. This is now the top rule for v8: the theme year's Halloween album gets its own reserved bucket, scored separately from rotation frequency. It also partially rehabilitates the “era-distinctiveness” idea that was rejected earlier — rejected correctly for the bulk slate, wrongly for era-signature set pieces.

Highway to Hell is the same failure from the other direction: 15 plays in 1994, ranked 32nd in the eligible pool, just outside the top-20 cut.

Tweezer: the conservative call was wrong

Tweezer went in at 5 points after a run-level analysis put it at 37% rather than the 49% a full commitment implied. It is the architecture of set two — a Tweezer → Down with Disease → Tweezer sandwich. The more aggressive placement at 8 points would have scored better. The reasoning behind the hedge was sound and the outcome went the other way; recorded here rather than quietly corrected.

Openers may simply be unwinnable

Backtested against nights 1 and 2, the opener model ranked the actual opener 14th and 3rd, and no variant improved it. Tonight it was a 485-show bustout. Three data points, no model in reach: this category should probably be reframed or dropped rather than tuned.

Why the ladder is ranked strictly by probability

The game scores Σ points × P(played). By the rearrangement inequality that is maximised by pairing the largest point value with the largest probability, straight down — the ranking is the entire strategy. 10 + 9 + 8 = 27 of 55 points sit in the top three slots, so half the game is decided by three songs. Tonight all three hit.

The probabilities were calibrated first: summed play rates predicted 8.77 hits on #92 (actual 9) and 8.41 on #93 (actual 7), a ratio of 0.93 — so raw play rate is already a well-calibrated probability and needed no fudge factor.

Dormancy: still do not filter on it

Both earlier nights ran a median gap of 10–11 shows with 3–4 songs past a 300-show gap. Tonight fits exactly: a current-rotation core plus Back in the U.S.S.R. (485) and Highway to Hell (659). A gap penalty would have thrown away the show's two most memorable moments — and no gap-based model could have picked them either.

Why this page is not auto-polling

Live results are baked in and re-published rather than fetched in the browser. Auto-polling would require the phish.net API key in the page source of a public repository, which would leak it. The clean path to genuine live updates is the existing setlist bot's phishpicks ingest endpoint, which already receives one JSON record per song and needs no key on the read side.
