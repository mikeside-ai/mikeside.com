How the picks for nights 3–5 are made model v7 · measured, not estimated

This model got simpler because the data said it should. Every earlier version — including the one that was on this page four hours ago — was tuned on intuition about what a themed night “feels like.” With the phish.net API wired in, the actual play counts are now measured: 1994 alone is 147 shows, 3,084 song rows, 340 distinct songs.

The measurement killed both the old model and its replacement. What survives is the dullest possible rule, and it beats everything else tested.

The error that ran through every previous version

v5 concluded that setlists draw from the middle of a year's frequency ranking, and built its picks around a “20–60% depth band.” That was an artifact of ranking each song within the full theme-year pool instead of the eligible pool — the one that remains after striking everything already played earlier in the run.

A correction to this page's own last update

Earlier today this model was revised to reserve three slots for “era-signature covers,” on the reasoning that night 2 played six covers so v5 was wrong to exclude them. The play counts say v5 was right and that revision was wrong. Covers do get played, but no individual cover is a good standalone bet — the pool is enormous and flat, exactly as v5 argued. Amazing Grace is on tonight's slate purely because it was played 31 times in 1994, which is how a cover should earn a slot: on frequency, not on a reserved quota. Loving Cup, Frankenstein and the Purple Rain vacuum solo were cut. So was Wolfman's Brother, promoted this afternoon on the reasoning that Hoist is the 1994 record — true, but it was barely played live that year.

What the model is now, in full

1. Pull every song played in the theme year from phish.net; count plays. 2. Strike every song already played earlier in this run. 3. Take the top 20 by play count. That's it — no depth band, no cover quota, no archetype buckets, no simulation.

The run no-repeat rule in step 2 is the only real insight here, and it's free: 7/22 and 7/24 share zero songs, and 50 are now off the board for tonight.

The honest caveat

One scored blind night is the entire evidentiary base, and v7's measured margin over random on it is 1.2 songs out of 20. That is well inside noise for a single show. The strongest claim available is that v7 is simpler than what it replaced, is fitted to nothing, and would have beaten the published slate on the only night both could be scored against. Judge it across nights 3, 4 and 5 — and expect scores to drift upward regardless, because the eligible pool shrinks every night whether or not the model is any good.
