Phish @ MSG — Setlist Predictions

5 NIGHTS · #92–96

Five-night Madison Square Garden run celebrating Phish's 92nd through 96th shows at the Garden — each night themed to a year, 1992 → 1996. Twenty predicted songs per night; check them off as they're played. Everything here is editable — click any song to rename it, use ✕ to remove, + add to insert your own. Each night has its own list and its own Reset. Predictions never reuse a song that was actually played earlier in the run — Phish doesn't repeat across a residency, so every song confirmed played on an earlier night is struck from the pool for every night after it.

How the picks for nights 3–5 are made model v5 · 100,000 simulated shows

Nights 3, 4 and 5 were rebuilt after running about 100,000 simulated shows against the two setlists we can actually check. Every rule below was fully specified before it saw a night, then fitted on one real show and scored on the other — so the numbers are held out, not fitted-and-graded on the same data.

Two ideas got cut. Reserving a quarter of the slots for era-appropriate covers turned out to be negative value — a cover slot is worth about 0.10 expected hits against 0.32 for a rotation slot, because the cover pool is enormous and flat. Covers get played; no individual cover is a good bet. And weighting songs by how distinctive they are to the theme year looked strong until it was tested properly: fitted on one real night and scored on the other it managed 12/40, worse than simply ranking by play count.

What survived is duller and works better. Both real setlists drew from the middle of the theme year's ranking — median rank 29 of 101 on Jul 22, 41 of 83 on Jul 24, with only about 7 of ~25 songs coming from the top 20 either night. So each slate takes a few songs from the head of that year's rotation and spreads the rest evenly through the 20–60% depth band. Sweeping that head/depth split across all 21 possible settings, every setting from 0 to 17 head picks beat a straight top-20 — the advantage is the shape, not any one split. Rather than trust the single best split, which on a two-show sample is noise, tonight's slate votes across the whole family and keeps the 20 songs most settings agree on.

The honest caveat: three scored shows is a tiny sample, the fitted depth curve failed its own cross-validation as a standalone predictor, and the eligible pool shrinks every night as songs get burned — so accuracy should climb through the run for reasons that have nothing to do with the model getting smarter.

Set placement below is cosmetic. The model predicts which twenty songs, not the running order — Set 1 / Set 2 / Encore is a human guess laid over the twenty, and scoring only counts whether the song was played at all.
