Run scorecard — what was caught, and what that's worth two live nights
Read this before the numbers

Only two of the three played nights had a published slate. Night 1 predates this project — nothing was preserved — so its bar below is a retrodiction, computed afterwards. It is not evidence of anything and is not counted in the live total.

The live record is 16 of 40 across two nights, against a random baseline of 7.4. Every other number on this page that beats it is fitted.

Why “beats random” is a weaker claim than it sounds

The comparator throughout is a random 20 songs drawn from the same eligible pool. That is a fair pool — but it is not the baseline a competent fan represents. The natural human baseline is “the twenty most-played songs of the theme year, minus whatever they already played this run” — and that is v7. Beating random therefore demonstrates two things a phish.net-literate fan already had for free: the theme-year premise, which the band announced publicly, and the no-repeat rule.

The model's edge over an informed fan is currently indistinguishable from zero, because the model is what an informed fan would do. Every cleverer idea tried this week — distinctiveness weighting, mid-depth sampling, archetype buckets, a cover quota — lost to the plain frequency list.

Scores will rise over the next two nights whatever happens

66 songs are now burned. The eligible pool shrinks every night, so a random slate gets better too. Expect #95 and #96 to score higher than #94 for arithmetic reasons that have nothing to do with the model improving. Judge v8 against the random baseline for its own night, not against Saturday.

And v8's 12/20 is worth nothing at all. It was produced by removing three picks already known to have missed and inserting two songs already known to have hit. Any rule built that way scores higher. It is printed here only so Monday's real number has something to be compared against.

What each night actually taught

#92 → run no-repeat. The played nights share zero songs. Striking every played song from later pools costs nothing and is the most valuable rule in the model. The cold blind guess for #93 collapsed to 2/20 precisely because six of its twenty picks had been played the night before.

#93 → rank inside the eligible pool, never the full year. v5 built a “mid-depth band” on an artifact of ranking within the full theme-year pool instead of the post-burn pool. Corrected, hit density falls monotonically with rank. Worth remembering: that same v5 was sold on this page as “held out, not fitted” at 19/40 — and then scored 6/20 live. That is the best evidence available for how much to trust any backtest here, including v8's.

#94 → the costume-album rule, on one observation. The night opened with Back in the U.S.S.R., off the Beatles' White Album, which Phish played as their 1994 Halloween musical costume.

The costume rule is n = 1

An earlier version of this page claimed the rule “fires for exactly the years that have one” and listed 1992 and 1993 as confirmations. Those are not confirmations. Neither year has a costume album, so the rule makes no prediction on those nights and nothing could have falsified it. The evidence is a single observation — and it is the same observation that inspired the rule.

A further hedge worth naming: v8 carries both readings of the rule for Monday — the repertoire pick (Drowned) and the album-opener pick (The Real Me). Two guesses at one idea means either landing will look like validation. That roughly doubles the chance of a spurious confirmation, and it is a deliberate trade for coverage rather than a clean test.

“Era-narrative wins the marquee slots” has a live record of 0 for 4

Every called shot on #94 missed — Wilson, Sleeping Monkey, Poor Heart, You Enjoy Myself. The narrative reading of that night (Halloween costume, Fish vacuum bit, Tweezerfest) is an explanation fitted afterwards, not a prediction that landed. v8 spends 3 of 20 slate slots on it anyway, which re-admits an idea this project measured and rejected a day earlier. That is a bet, not a finding, and Monday is the first time it is exposed.

Still standing

Do not filter on dormancy — each night runs a current-rotation core plus 3–4 deep bustouts. Secret Language is collinear with play count and adds nothing. Skipping the obvious loses hits on the bulk slate every way it was cut. Bustouts are worth predicting as a count, though “3–4” is a range drawn around the only three observations that exist, so a hit on Monday proves little.
