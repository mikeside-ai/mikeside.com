How the picks for nights 3–5 are made model v7 · measured, not estimated

This model got simpler because the data said it should. Every earlier version — including the one on this page a few hours ago — was tuned on intuition about what a themed night “feels like.” With the phish.net API wired in, the play counts are now measured: 1994 is 124 Phish shows and 340 distinct songs.

The measurement killed both the old model and its replacement. What survives is the dullest possible rule, and it beats everything else tested.

The error that ran through every previous version

v5 concluded that setlists draw from the middle of a year's frequency ranking, and built its picks around a “20–60% depth band.” That was an artifact of ranking each song within the full theme-year pool instead of the eligible pool — the one left after striking everything already played earlier in the run.

The four called shots

Each is drawn from its own measured distribution over the theme year, restricted to songs not yet burned. Opener — most frequent set-one opener. Encore — most frequent song in the first encore slot. Bustout — highest theme-year play count among songs whose current gap is 50 shows or more. Longest jam — most phish.net jamchart entries that year, which tracks where the extended playing actually happened rather than where it theoretically could.

These are honest long shots and the hit rate beside each says so. Openers are the least concentrated call on the board: the most common 1994 opener led only 9 of 124 shows, so 7% is the realistic ceiling, not a confident prediction. Encores concentrate a little better.

Worth watching: the calls and the 20-song slate disagree. Sleeping Monkey led 1994 encores eleven times but ranks 27th by overall play count, so it misses the top-20 cut entirely. Buried Alive is the second-most-common 1994 opener and isn't on the slate either. That disagreement is left visible rather than papered over — if the calls beat the slate on songs the slate rejected, the ranking rule is missing positional information, and that's a real finding.

A correction to this page's own earlier update

Earlier today this model reserved three slots for “era-signature covers,” reasoning that night 2 played six covers so v5 was wrong to exclude them. The play counts say v5 was right and that revision was wrong. Covers get played, but no individual cover is a good standalone bet — the pool is enormous and flat. Amazing Grace is on tonight's slate purely because it was played 31 times in 1994, which is how a cover should qualify: on frequency, not a quota. Loving Cup, Frankenstein and the Purple Rain vacuum solo were cut, as was Wolfman's Brother — promoted this afternoon because Hoist is the 1994 record, which is true and irrelevant, since it was barely played live that year. Album year is not play frequency.

A data error worth recording

The phish.net year feed includes side-project shows — Mike Gordon sit-ins, Jiggle the Handle dates — so an unfiltered pull reported 147 “1994 shows” when Phish played 124. Every count here now filters on artist_name == "Phish". The top 20 was unaffected, but the denominator was wrong, which quietly deflated every per-show rate on the page.

What writing the reasons exposed — the next thing to fix

Attaching a sourced rationale to all 60 picks surfaced a blind spot. v7 ranks on theme-year plays and completely ignores whether a song is anywhere near current rotation. Tonight is mostly fine — 16 of 20 picks have been played within the last dozen shows — but Amazing Grace carries a 900-show gap. Nights 4 and 5 are far more exposed: Acoustic Army hasn't appeared in 1,096 shows, Suspicious Minds 1,019, Johnny B. Goode 926, I'm Blue I'm Lonesome 791.

This is deliberately not fixed yet, because the right answer is unknown. A year-themed night is exactly the occasion a band reaches for dormant era material — night 2 proved it, opening the vault for Big Ball Jam at a 1,170-show gap and the first Kung since 2019. A dormancy penalty might improve the model or might destroy the thing that makes themed nights predictable at all. Tonight is the first test: if Amazing Grace lands, dormancy is noise; if the deep cuts miss across nights 3–5, v8 gets a gap term.

The honest caveat

One scored blind night is the entire evidentiary base, and v7's measured margin on it is 1.2 songs out of 20 — well inside noise. The strongest available claim is that v7 is simpler than what it replaced, is fitted to nothing, and would have beaten the published slate on the only night both could be scored against.

Set placement is cosmetic. The slate predicts which twenty songs, not the running order, and slate scoring counts only whether a song was played at all. The four called shots are scored separately and strictly.
