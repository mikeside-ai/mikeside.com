What a test is, and why these are honest

Every model is a deterministic rule executed blind — no hand-picking, no knowledge of the setlist. Play counts come only from shows strictly before the target date, so nothing leaks backwards. The burn list is the actual set of songs played on earlier nights of that same run.

The comparator is 20 random picks from the identical eligible pool. Across the ten shows that expects 30.3 hits; the best model got 90.

What this changes

1. The reserved slots are a real cost, now measured twice. v8 loses 4 hits to plain v7 across ten shows, on top of losing on #94. The mechanism buys songs frequency cannot reach — and pays more than they are worth.

2. The probabilities are wrong by a factor of 2.6, everywhere. Not one test came in under its predicted ladder score. A per-show play rate badly understates the chance a song appears, because the band draws roughly 24 songs from a pool of 170 and the head of that pool is not sampled uniformly. This is fixable arithmetic and it is the highest-value repair available.

3. Deep bustouts are unreachable, confirmed. Nine songs with a 300-plus show gap were played across the ten tests. All four models caught zero, in forty attempts. Predicting them by name is not a hard problem, it is the wrong problem — which is what the bustout-as-a-count reframe already assumed.

4. 60% of what Phish plays is out of reach of any frequency rule. Of 225 songs played, only 90 sat inside the top-20 band. v7 caught all 90 and nothing else. Beating 40% coverage requires a fundamentally different signal, not a better sort.
