What a test is, and why these are honest

Every model is a deterministic rule executed blind — no hand-picking, no knowledge of the setlist. Play counts come only from shows strictly before the target date, so nothing leaks backwards. The burn list is the actual set of songs played on earlier nights of that same run.

The comparator is 20 random picks from the identical eligible pool. Across the hundred shows that expects 309 hits; v9 got 828.

What this changes — v9 is released

1. The 10-show finding was wrong, and 100 shows corrected it. On ten shows, rotation state appeared to flip sign between staples and marginal songs. At 7,536 candidates that vanishes: recently-played songs are suppressed in every frequency band, cleanly and monotonically. The earlier model built on the sign flip failed its holdout. This one does not.

2. v9 ships as frequency × rotation state, calibrated. Multipliers by due-ness: 0.63 under half a typical gap, 1.14, 1.32, 1.30. No reserved slots — those were measured losing twice and are gone. Probabilities pass through a calibration curve fitted on 50 shows and validated on the other 50.

3. Be honest about the size of the win. Per show, v9 versus v7 is 34 wins, 33 losses, 33 ties. The edge is +1.7% on coverage, +6.7% on ladder points, and a genuine drop in variance. It is a better ranker, not a better finder.

4. The ceiling has not moved. Roughly 60% of what Phish plays still sits outside any frequency rule's reach, and 368 songs were unreachable in principle. Getting past that needs a different signal — segue affinity and set-position priors are next.
