What a test is, and why these are honest

Every model is a deterministic rule executed blind — no hand-picking, no knowledge of the setlist. Play counts come only from shows strictly before the target date, so nothing leaks backwards. The burn list is the actual set of songs played on earlier nights of that same run.

The comparator is 20 random picks from the identical eligible pool. Across the hundred shows that expects 309 hits; v9 got 828, v7 806, v8 765.

What this changes

1. v8 is settled, and it is a loss. 765 against v7's 806 — 41 songs given up, 43 shows lost to 11 won. Three separate measurements now agree: #94, the 10-show backtest, and 100 simulations. Reserved slots are a bad trade and they are gone from v9.

2. v6, v7 and v8 have identical ladders. All three post 2,741 and 473 top-ten hits, because they share the same frequency head and differ only in slate slots 15–20 — which score nothing on a ten-pick ladder. For the setlist game specifically, the choice between them is irrelevant.

3. Reserving slots for a category works, and still loses. v6 catches 193 covers, the most of any model. It also finishes 107 songs behind v7. That is the general shape of every clever idea tried on this project.

4. v9's edge is real and small. 37 wins, 28 losses, 35 ties against v7. +22 hits, +142 ladder points, and a tighter spread. It is a better ranker, not a better finder — roughly 60% of what Phish plays is still out of reach of any frequency rule.
