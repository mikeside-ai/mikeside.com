What a test is, and why these are honest

Every model is a deterministic rule executed blind — no hand-picking, no knowledge of the setlist. Play counts come only from shows strictly before the target date, so nothing leaks backwards. The burn list is the actual set of songs played on earlier nights of that same run. The panel was rebuilt on 29 July 2026 and is now the complete population — every show from 1994 to 2026 that is night 3 or later of a consecutive-night run, 394 of them, rather than a 100-show sample that could not be reproduced. Songs are keyed on phish.net song IDs, not display titles, which removes the era-alias problem at source: Taste That Surrounds and Taste are one song by construction.

What “Chance” means: the comparator is 20 blind picks from the identical eligible pool — pure arithmetic, not a model. Across the 394 shows that expects 1,626 hits; Forbin v10 and v11 got 2,573, Forbin v9 2,565, Forbin v7 2,538, Forbin v5 2,039.

What this changes

1. The panel was rebuilt, and every number on this page changed with it. The original 100 shows were a sample whose selection could not be reconstructed. This panel is the complete population — all 394 shows from 1994 to 2026 that are night 3 or later of a consecutive run — so it is reproducible, extensible, and 4× the evidence. It reproduced the MSG run's burn list, pool size, all four night lengths and all twenty of tonight's play counts exactly, which is the check that it is measuring the right thing.

2. Forbin v11's depletion term is withdrawn, one day after release. It fixed aggregate calibration and made per-show calibration worse — 0.674 mean error against 0.500 without it — and its anchor constant moved 1.60 → 2.14 purely because the sample grew. It never changed a ranking, so nothing is lost but the odds, which are now the plain knot values.

3. The model's edge collapses as the catalogue burns — 3.24× chance on a full pool, 1.34× on a pool the size of tonight's. This is the opposite of v11's premise and it is the most useful thing the rebuild found. A depleted pool makes each survivor likelier and the slate weaker, because the burn list eats exactly the material a frequency model is good at.

4. The rotation signal is an arch, not a slope. Long-past-due songs are suppressed as hard as recently-played ones (0.61 either end). Forbin v9 only ever modelled one side. Adding the far side is Forbin v12, which gains 163 ladder points and holds up on a clean 50/50 holdout. Queued behind tonight rather than shipped mid-run.

5. On curated nights the recency penalty vanishes — lift 0.98 against 0.61 on ordinary shows. Vlad made that correction by hand on #95 when he restored Prince Caspian on a 3-show gap; it hit. The measurement now agrees with him. His corrections stand at 6 for 6; the model's called shots at 0 for 8.

6. Six versions in, plain frequency still does nearly all the work. Forbin v7 to v12 spans 35 songs out of 7,788 — 1.4%. Barbells, cover quotas, reserved slots, filler indices, song families and depletion scaling have all been measured and all but one have lost. The gains that survived are small, one-sided, and about honesty rather than cleverness.

The pick for #96

Forbin v11.2 — which on a curated night is the Forbin v7 ranking.

The choice collapses before the data gets a vote, and that is the cleanest argument for it. Forbin v9 adds one thing to Forbin v7: a penalty on recently-played songs. Forbin v10 switches that penalty off on curated nights. Forbin v11 changed only the odds, and v11.2 withdrew that change. So on a residency night v7, v10, v11 and v11.2 all emit the identical twenty songs in the identical order — there is no choice to make between them. The panel confirms it arithmetically: across the 24 occasion shows, Forbin v7 and Forbin v10 both average 12.29 hits. Not close — equal.

That leaves two real alternatives, and both are measurably worse here.

Forbin v9 — rejected, and this run tested it directly. Its recency penalty is the one thing it adds, and on curated nights that penalty is measured at a lift of 0.98: no effect at all. It is not neutral in practice, it is harmful, because it removes real picks. #95 is a live test of exactly this. The rotation term suppressed Prince Caspian on a 3-show gap; it was restored by hand over the model's objection, and it hit — as did Billy Breathes and NICU, which the same suppression would have removed. Forbin v9 would have scored 5 of 20 on #95 where Forbin v10 scored 8. The panel says the same thing more quietly: 11.96 against 12.29 on occasion shows, the only model that gets worse on these nights.

Forbin v12 — validated, but not tonight. It is the best model on the panel overall and leads the ladder by 163 points, so this is a genuinely close call. Two things hold it back. On the 24 occasion shows specifically it is behind: 290 hits against Forbin v10's 295, with the ladder dead level — its edge is on ordinary shows, not these. And its mechanism is far-dormant suppression, which on a 1996-themed show played in 2026 hits a case the panel has never seen: every theme-year song looks "overdue" against a 2026 baseline while its play rate is a 1996 rate. The two are not commensurable. Using it tonight would be extrapolating past the evidence, which is the specific error the last two releases were written to correct.

Forbin v5 is out on every measure — last on the panel by 500 songs, and the weakest live night of the run at 1.8×.

Confidence, stated honestly. Three independent lines agree — the structural argument that the candidates are the same object, the 394-case panel, and the live run — but they are not fully independent of each other, and the live run is four shows. The strongest claim available is that the choice between v7, v10, v11 and v11.2 is not a choice, and that the two models that would differ tonight both measure worse on nights like tonight. That is a narrow claim, and it is the one the evidence actually supports.

Forbin v11 / v11.1superseded — measured on the old 100-show panel29 Jul 2026

A calibration release. The ranking is Forbin v10's plus one data fix; everything else here is about the numbers printed next to the picks.

  • Title aliasing — the only ranking change, worth +3 hits. 1995 logs the song as Taste That Surrounds, 1996 and after log it as Taste. Treated as two songs, the burn list misses it and the gap reads 1088 when the song is current at 7. 81 of the 100 cases touch this alias. Parent/child too: playing The Curtain With now retires The Curtain. 836 → 839. Ladder unchanged at 2,866.
  • The 1.4× occasion uplift is gone, replaced by a measured depletion term. The uplift was a proxy and a poor one. What actually makes picks likelier late in a run is that the pool shrinks: a depletion factor D = expected show length ÷ the summed play rates of everything still eligible. Across the 100 cases D runs 1.23 to 3.64, median 1.85. Probabilities scale by D.
  • v11 anchored D on a single show, and 100 shows moved it. The released v11 divided by 1.97, a constant read off #95 alone. Backtested, that anchor predicts 0.813 hits for every hit that lands — a systematic 19% under-call. Refitting gives 1.60: ratio 1.001 across all 100, and it holds out — fit on the even 50 → 0.952 on the odd 50, fit on the odd 50 → 1.053, fit on the earliest 50 → 1.092 on the latest 50. This is the third time in this project a confident structural story built on one show did not survive a hundred.
  • Tested and rejected: a run's own prior nights as a length prior. #95 played 20 songs where the occasion prior assumed 27.1, which suggested using the run's own nights. Measured, that prior is 22% worse than the class average — MAE 4.79 against 3.92 — and the correlation between a run's earlier nights and its target night is only 0.188. Show length is very nearly independent night to night.
  • Tested and rejected: song-family propagation. The conditionals are enormous and real — Forbin's Ascent → Mockingbird 100%, Swept Away → Steep 100%, The Horse → Silent 99%, Hydrogen → Weekapaug 98%. Forcing a pick's ≥90% partner into the slate changed 32 of 100 cases and lost 4 hits. The reason is worth keeping: the conditional is P(partner | anchor played), but a slate holds the anchor as a prediction. P(partner | anchor predicted) is just P(partner), which frequency already knows — so the forced slot only displaces an independently-ranked pick. Same shape as the reserved-slot failure.
  • Tested and rejected: a two-anchor split for occasion nights. Occasion shows want an anchor of 1.32 and ordinary shows 1.76, which looks like a residual occasion effect surviving depletion. But the occasion anchor is fitted on 28 shows — 14 per holdout half — and moves 1.19 to 1.44 across splits, while the ordinary anchor sits at 1.76 in every split. The second parameter does not earn its keep.
  • Known weakness, stated plainly. In the top decile of depletion (D 2.5–3.6) the ten shows in that band delivered 12.1 hits on average while the refitted anchor calls 9.5 — still a 22% under-call. That band would prefer an anchor near 1.20, but ten shows is exactly the sample size this release exists to warn about. The 78% cap has no backtest behind it either: it never binds across the 100 cases.
Forbin v10superseded27 Jul 2026

Occasion-aware. Two changes, both confined to special-occasion nights — NYE, Halloween, Baker's Dozen and this MSG run.

  • Rotation-state term switched off on curated nights. A residency does not avoid recently-played songs; the only songs out of play are the ones burned earlier in the same run. On the 28 occasion shows Forbin v9 was scoring below plain Forbin v7 (298 against 303) because of this. Forbin v10 scores 306.
  • Probabilities lifted 1.4× on occasion nights, capped at 78%. Those nights average 27.1 songs against 21.1, so every pick is likelier to land. Calibration goes 1.56 → 1.24. No picks change.
  • Tested and rejected: a “shorter songs” ranking bias built on a filler index. It finds the right songs but gives no edge — see chart 8.
