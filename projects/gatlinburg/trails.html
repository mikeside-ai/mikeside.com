Walks, not hikes

Trails the whole crew can do

Under five miles round trip, no sustained steep grades, for ages one to seven and knees over sixty

Sarah Stierch · CC0
Sun 22Mon 23Tue 24Wed 25Thu 26Fri 27Sat 28BonusTrails

Every walk here was chosen with the slowest person in mind. The kid-and-grandparent score is ours: five dots means a two-year-old walks most of it and nobody's knees complain; two dots means it's worth it but somebody will be carried or will want to stop. Every one of these needs the $15 weekly parking tag on the dash. Pack the carrier; the stroller only works where it says so.

Kid and grandparent score ●●●●●

An old road along the Little Pigeon, flat, wide, and one of two trails in the park that take dogs. Walk twenty minutes out and back and you've had a hike; walk the whole thing and you're at the Gatlinburg Bypass. Good on a damp day because gravel doesn't turn to mud.

Kid and grandparent score ●●●●●

The old logging railbed, so the grade never steepens. Follows the Little River the entire way with pools and boulders to look at. Turn around at any point; the small cascade at Huskey Branch is 2.3 miles in and is the reward if the kids have it in them. Grandparent-friendly footing.

Best slot: The spare morning

Kid and grandparent score ●●●●●

From the picnic tables by the river to a one-room 1882 schoolhouse with a cemetery. Short rolling hills, lots of roots, nothing steep. The picnic area itself, with the shallow river, is worth the stop even if nobody walks. The Walker Sisters cabin adds another 1.1 miles each way; skip it with the little two.

Best slot: Wednesday on the way back from Cades Cove

The best waterfall-per-effort ratio in the park: a wide old road climbs gently beside the river and the long Lynn Camp Prong cascades start at 0.6 miles. A 1930s Cadillac rusting in the woods sits at 2.3 miles if Cash wants a goal. The last three miles of road to the trailhead are gravel.
