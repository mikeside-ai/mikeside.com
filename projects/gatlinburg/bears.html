Bears

Where to look, when to look, what to do when you find one, and a shared log so the whole family keeps score.

NPS · Public domain
Sun 22Mon 23Tue 24Wed 25Thu 26Fri 27Sat 28BonusTrailsFishingCabinBears

The Smokies hold roughly two black bears per square mile, the densest population in the East, and late November is the tail end of their eating season. They're out at dawn and dusk stuffing themselves on the last acorns and walnuts before they den in December, and a warm week like this one keeps them out longer. So the odds are real, they're best early and late, and they're best from the car. Below: nine places ranked by our odds, a map, the rules that keep a sighting a good story, and a log that every phone shares.

Cades Cove Loop Directions

Wednesday on the plan · about an hour each way

When
First hour after the gate opens, or the last before dark
Where
Sparks Lane, Hyatt Lane, the fields past Cable Mill, the walnut and cherry trees along the loop
From the car
Yes. The car is your blind
Stroller
Fine on the paved pull-offs; not needed

Bear odds ●●●●●

The best odds in the park, and it's already on the agenda. In November the bears are eating everything left on the trees and the ground, so look up into the big walnuts and cherries along the loop as much as across the fields. A row of stopped cars with people out of them is a bear jam: pull fully off, stay by the car, and don't walk toward it. Cash counts bears and deer; whoever's driving watches the road, not the field.

Best slot: the 7:15 departure that's already on Wednesday. The last loop before dark is nearly as good and the cove is empty

When
Dusk, the last hour the road is open
Where
Creek crossings, the Ogle place, the Ephraim Bales cabin
From the car
Yes, one way, 10 mph, pull-offs
Closes
Nov 30 for the winter, so this is the last week

Bear odds ●●●●

The in-town answer. Five and a half slow one-way miles through old-growth woods and creek bottoms ten minutes from the cabin, and reviewers' bear reports here run all fall. It closes for the season the day after you leave. Put the littles in pajamas and run it as the after-dinner drive on any evening that's dry; you're back at the cabin forty minutes later with two kids asleep.

Best slot: Tuesday or Friday at 4:15, so you're out before the 5:25 dark. Also Wednesday's Plan B

Any time the tram runs
Where
The bear habitat at the top, next to the tubing hill
Guaranteed
Yes. These bears live here

Bear odds ●●●●●

The sure thing, and it's already Thanksgiving morning. Resident black bears in a habitat at the top of the tram, close enough that Snejana can see one, with the tubing hill and the coaster beside it. It's the only entry on this page where the bear is a certainty, and for a one- and a two-year-old it may be the one that lands. Everything else here is a maybe at dawn.

Elkmont and Little River Road Directions

On the way to Cades Cove · 20 min from the cabin

When
Dawn and dusk, both directions
Where

From the car
Yes

Bear odds ●●●

You drive this road twice on Wednesday and twice more on the way in and out, so it's free odds. Slow down through the cove hardwoods between Sugarlands and Metcalf Bottoms and watch the river side. The Elkmont spur is a five-minute detour; bears walk the empty campground loop in the off-season.

Best slot: the Wednesday drive out at 7:15 and the drive back after naps

Chimneys and Newfound Gap Road Directions

Friday on the plan, on the way to Kuwohi · 20 min from the cabin

When
Morning, on the way up
Where
Pull-offs between Sugarlands and the Chimneys, the picnic area if the gate is open
From the car
Yes
Note
Above the Chimneys it gets too high and cold for bears in late November

Bear odds ●●

The road up to Newfound Gap is bear country for its first eight miles, then it climbs out of it. Watch the river side and the oak slopes on the way up Friday; the pull-offs are wide and the picnic area, when open, is a classic spot. By Kuwohi you're looking for ravens, not bears.

Best slot: Friday on the way up, 8 to 9, before the sun's on the road

Gatlinburg Trail from Sugarlands Directions

Tuesday on the plan · 10 min from the cabin

When
First light
Where
The river side of the trail, the old homesite chimneys
On foot
Flat gravel, two miles each way, stroller fine
Note
The one trail in the park that allows dogs, so bears keep their distance in the daytime

Bear odds ●●

A river corridor straight out of town. Bears cross it at dawn before the dog walkers arrive, then make themselves scarce. Low odds on a walk, but it's the walk you're already doing Tuesday, and the rangers at the visitor center post the week's sightings on a board inside. Ask them where the bears have been.

Best slot: ask at the Sugarlands desk Tuesday morning, then decide

Mynatt Park and the edge of town Directions

Off the Parkway at the south end · 5 min from the cabin

When
Dusk
Where
The creek through the park, Ski Mountain Road, the first mile of the Spur
From the car
Yes, or the playground with an adult watching the creek
Note
Town bears are the bold ones. Trash rules matter here

Bear odds ●●●

Gatlinburg has a town bear population that lives on the ridges between the cabins, and the places they show are the creek corridors and the roads at dusk: Mynatt Park, Ski Mountain Road up to Ober, the Spur toward Pigeon Forge, and Herbert Holt Park, where fishing reviewers report one most weeks. These are the bears that have learned about trash and open car doors, so admire them from the car and keep the rules from the cabin page.

Best slot: a dusk drive home from downtown, up Ski Mountain Road and back

    So it stays a good story

    The rules

    50 yardsHalf a football field. Getting closer than that on purpose is illegal in the park, and if the bear changes what it's doing because of you, you're too close whatever the distance.
    Stay in the carFor a roadside bear the car is the perfect blind. Windows down, engine on, pull fully off the road. Nobody gets out, and nobody opens a door on the bear's side.
    Never feed, never leave foodA fed bear becomes a bold bear and is often destroyed. Fines start at thousands of dollars. That includes the snack Slavik dropped at the picnic table.
    Kids upOn a trail, the two littles go in carriers and Cash walks between adults the moment a bear is seen. Group up, look big, talk in a normal voice.
    Don't runBack away slowly, the way you came. Running starts a chase, and a bear is faster than anyone in this family, uphill or down.
    If it keeps comingShout, wave arms, throw rocks, make yourselves bigger and louder together. Black bears that approach are almost always after food, not you. If one attacks, fight back; don't play dead.
    Bear jamsA line of stopped cars on the loop is a bear. Don't pass, don't honk, don't walk up. Watch from where you are.
    ReportA bear that comes into a picnic area or campsite, or follows people, gets reported to a ranger or at Sugarlands. In town, call the police non-emergency line, or the host for the cabin.

    Not this trip: Cataloochee is two hours each way and is the elk valley, not a bear spot. Laurel Falls is closed to 2027. Nothing on this page needs a hike longer than the ones on the trails page.
