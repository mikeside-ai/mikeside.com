Family trip · Great Smoky Mountains, Tennessee

Thanksgiving week in Gatlinburg

Seven days in a cabin with a seven-year-old, a two-year-old and a one-year-old. Out early, nap hard, lights after dark, home by seven. Everything below is built around that rhythm and around the two days the whole town shows up.

Read this before anything else

Dollywood is closed on Thanksgiving Day. It runs 10 to 9 Sunday through Wednesday, closes Thursday, then opens 9 to 10 Friday and Saturday for what is reliably the two busiest days of the Christmas season. So Dollywood goes on Monday. Thursday is Ober and dinner. Friday you stay out of town entirely and go into the park.

Kuwohi Road, Roaring Fork and Little Greenbrier Road all close for the winter on November 30. This is the last week they are open. Laurel Falls is closed for rebuild until 2027, so cross it off. The park needs a parking tag for any stop over 15 minutes. Buy the $15 weekly tag at Sugarlands on Tuesday and it covers the whole trip.

Gatlinburg is on Eastern time. Every time on this page is Eastern. Phones switch on their own near Chattanooga, car clocks don't.

Trailhead

Sunday's drive

I-59 north to Chattanooga, I-75 north to Lenoir City, then US-321 east through Maryville to Townsend and into the park. From the Townsend Wye, Little River Road runs 18 miles along the river to Sugarlands and drops you into Gatlinburg from the park side. That route skips Exit 407, Sevierville and the Pigeon Forge Parkway, which on a holiday Sunday afternoon are the slowest 15 miles in Tennessee. Little River Road is twisty. If anyone in the back gets carsick, take Wears Valley Road to Pigeon Forge instead and accept the Parkway.

Who sleeps where

Three bedrooms, sleeps ten. Downstairs king with its own bath and the clawfoot tub: the grandparents, and it doubles as the kids' bath. Upstairs king: Vlad and Allison, pack-n-play in the closet corner for Snejana. Queen log bunk room: Cash on top, Slavik on the bottom with the stair gate across the door. Loft futon: spare, and the loft rail is on the safety sweep. The dining table seats six and there are seven of you, so bring the booster and the travel high chair; neither is listed. Washer and dryer are in the cabin, so pack light on kid clothes. Reviewers see bears from the porch most weeks: trash goes inside, never on the deck, and no kid on the deck alone.

The hot tub is the actual risk on this trip

At the beach it was the pool. Here it is the hot tub on the deck, and it is worse, because it is right outside a sliding door, the cover is usually unlatched, and the water is hot enough to hurt a one-year-old in minutes. Within ten minutes of walking in: strap the cover down and keep it strapped unless an adult is in it, put a door alarm on every deck door, and check the deck rail spacing against Slavik's head. Then check the stairs and any loft rail. Cabins are stacked three stories on a hillside, and the stair gates they advertise are rarely there. If the driveway is steep and it freezes overnight, nobody carries a child down it. One adult is the designated watcher whenever the deck door is open, and you hand that job off out loud.

Who forges what

Iron Mountain's rules: the railroad spike knife is 13 and up, the horseshoe is 9 and up, the pony shoe is a smaller knife at $35, and the nail knife is 5 to 12 at $20. Cash qualifies for the nail knife only; don't argue it up a tier, the smith won't. Slavik and Snejana watch from behind the rope or walk the square with Allison. Sessions take 15 minutes of your hammering plus the smith's finishing; plan an hour for both of you. Reviewers' consensus is that this is the thing their kids talked about all the way home.

Bears are real in the Cove in November

They are fattening up before denning and they are on the loop. Fifty yards minimum, nobody gets out of the car for a bear, and no food left out at the picnic area. If a bear jam forms, drive through it. You will see another one.

If you want someone else to cook

Mama's Farmhouse, 208 Pickel St, Pigeon Forge Directions. Family-style, all you can eat, a special Thanksgiving menu, open 11 to 9. The right call for this crew because food arrives in bowls immediately and nobody needs a menu. Reservations are encouraged and it fills up. Book it today as the backup even if you plan to cook. Ober Mountain runs a Thanksgiving Feast at the top, 11 to 5, which would fold the whole morning into one trip; reservations there too. Cracker Barrel on the Pigeon Forge Parkway serves its traditional plate from 11 with no reservation, and The Park Grill in Gatlinburg has a special menu 11:30 to 9. The Pigeon Forge trolley doesn't run on Thanksgiving, so whatever you pick, you're driving.

Heights will decide Cash's day

No minimum: Dollywood Express, Village Carousel, Lemon Twist, Flying Elephants, Rockin' Roadway. Treetop Tower and Frogs and Fireflies need a 14+ companion under 42 and 36 inches. The Country Fair kiddie rides moved to a 36-inch minimum for 2026, so Slavik may be shut out of Busy Bees and Lucky Ducky. At 42 inches Cash gets Blazing Fury, Sky Rider and the Waltzing Swinger. At 48 inches he gets Thunderhead, Mystery Mine and Barnstormer. A seven-year-old is usually right around 48, so measure him at the cabin Sunday night and again at the park.

Backcountry plan

When it rains, freezes, or someone's sick

One day in three is wet in November, the high road closes with any ice, and the SkyBridge shuts in wind. Have these in your pocket so nobody has to invent a plan with three tired kids in a cabin.

Ripley's Aquarium of the Smokies

88 River Rd, downtown · from about $40, age 1 and under free

The best bad-weather pick for this age spread. Penguins, the shark tunnel on a moving walkway, a touch tank for Cash. Two hours, indoors, stroller-friendly, parking in the garage next door. Go at opening; by noon on a rainy day it's the whole town's idea.

Thanksgiving Arts & Crafts Show

Gatlinburg Convention Center · Nov 24–29 · free

Indoor, warm, wide aisles for the stroller, and the craftspeople are good with kids. An easy hour. Downtown parking garage across the street.

Sugarlands Visitor Center ★ 4.72k

US-441, 2 mi south of town · free

Natural history museum with the mounted animals, a short film, rangers who will talk to a seven-year-old about bears for as long as he wants. Cataract Falls is behind it and fine in light rain.

Winterfest lights from the car

Pigeon Forge Parkway · nightly · free

Five miles of lights along the Parkway. Pajamas, car seats, hot chocolate, radio. Works at 6pm when nothing else does. Shadrack's in Sevierville is the paid, better version.

Anakeesta in the rain ★ 4.218k

576 Parkway

Runs in rain, and the Chondola is enclosed, but the whole mountaintop is outdoors and the Skywalk closes in wind. Fine for a drizzle, miserable in a cold soak. Check the forecast before you commit $90.

Pigeon Forge Snow ★ 4.44k

2533 Teaster Ln · indoor · $29.99/hr tubing

Real snow tubing indoors, 38 inches to ride, so both boys, Slavik with an adult. Reviewers say the separate snow-play room is a slab of ice; skip that add-on. Snejana watches from the warm side with hot chocolate.

The Island in Pigeon Forge ★ 4.765k

131 The Island Dr · free parking and entry

Carousel, the big wheel, fountain show on the hour, a mile of lights, forty places to eat. Works in any weather that isn't a downpour, and costs nothing until you buy a wheel ticket.

Rainforest Adventures Zoo ★ 4.55k

109 Nascar Dr, Sevierville · 25 min · about $22

Toddler-height exhibits, fifty goats to feed, mostly indoors. Parents of two-year-olds rate it above the aquarium. Come-and-go wristbands all day.

Patriot Park and the Old Mill square ★ 4.64k

236 Old Mill Ave, Pigeon Forge · free

Riverside path, grass, the water wheel next door, ducks off the dam, the general store's candy counter. The no-ticket hour that saves an afternoon.

Skip: the Parkway on Fri or Sat

Any of it

Ripley's Believe It or Not, the mini golf, the pancake houses. Nothing on the strip is worth an hour of Black Friday crawl with a one-year-old. Save it for a future trip when they're all walking.

Three cubs

What each of them actually needs

Cash

7 years

  • His trip is tubing, skating, the coasters, the bears and the top of the state. One of those per day is his thing.
  • Give him a job. Deer counter, headlamp captain, wristband keeper. It cuts the whining in half.
  • He's the only one who can handle a full day out. Split up on purpose: one adult and Cash at the Ober rink, one adult and the little two in the lodge.
  • Measure him before you leave. Two inches is the difference between a great Dollywood day and a disappointed one.

Slavik

2, three in December

  • He's fast, he's fearless, and the cabin has a hot tub, three decks and open stairs. He's the reason for the gates, the alarms and the cover strap.
  • He tubes at Ober on an adult's lap, and that's his one thrill of the week. No chairlift. Bears and the rink from the rail fill the rest of the morning.
  • Two hours is his ceiling anywhere. Leave before he tells you he's done.
  • The nap is not optional. Every bad evening this week traces back to a skipped nap.

Snejana

1 year

  • She lives in the carrier this week. Strollers are banned at SkyPark and on the Skywalk, and useless at Kuwohi and Cataract Falls.
  • Cold hands and a cold face are the thing to watch. Check them every half hour outside. Mittens that stay on matter more than the coat.
  • She counts as a heartbeat on the SkyLift and needs a ticketed adult everywhere else. Plan on two chairs and two adults for everything.
  • The train at Dollywood and the bears at Ober are her two best moments. Everything else is tolerated.
  • Protect her sleep space hardest. That's what decides whether the adults enjoy this trip.
