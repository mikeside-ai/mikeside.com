For the grandparents

Trout fishing

Wild trout in the park, stocked rainbows in town, and one place that guarantees a fish. All within 45 minutes of the cabin.

Blinutne · CC BY-SA 3.0
Sun 22Mon 23Tue 24Wed 25Thu 26Fri 27Sat 28BonusTrailsFishingCabin

Two kinds of water, ten minutes apart. The national park has wild trout, artificial lures only, and no stocking. The city of Gatlinburg stocks rainbows every Thursday from its own trout farm and lets you use bait and keep five. Late November is cold-water fishing: the fish are there, they are just slow. Fish the warm middle of the day and go to town water if the goal is dinner.

The short version. Buy licenses online tonight. Park water for a quiet morning and scenery. Town water or the trout farm when you want to actually catch something. No fishing anywhere in town on Thursday, which this week is Thanksgiving anyway.

Paperwork

Licenses, bought from the couch

Everything is sold at GoOutdoorsTennessee.com or the TWRA On The Go app. You need a Social Security number to buy. Screenshot the license; cell service in the park is thin. Kids 12 and under fish free anywhere. There is no senior discount for out-of-state anglers, so over-65 pays the same as anyone.

Tennessee residents with a driver's license have a cheaper menu, including a senior license at 65. Prices above are TWRA's posted fees and skip the small processing charge. Town permits are also sold at Gatlinburg City Hall on East Parkway and the Spur Welcome Center.

Rules

Park water versus town water

HoursHalf hour before sunrise to half hour after sunset.Same. Closed every Thursday for stocking.

Do not move or stack rocks in the park; it is illegal and it kills the bugs the trout eat. Do not dump fish guts near a campsite. On Dec 1 the town goes catch-and-release, but we are gone by then.

The city trout farm is here, so this is the most reliably fishy water in town. Postcard water: plunge pools between boulders the size of cars, and picnic tables within sight of the rod. Fish it from the rocks rather than wading, because the pools are deep and the rocks are slick. Dap a nymph into the pockets. The most fished stream in the park, and the one with the biggest browns, which are spawning right now. The Little River Trail is a flat gravel road along the water, so a grandparent can walk ten minutes, pick a pool, and have it alone on a November weekday. Stay out of the clean, pale gravel patches at the tails of pools; those are brown trout nests. The Elkmont Nature Trail on the trails page is the same trailhead, so this pairs with a family walk.

Best slot: Wednesday on the way back from Cades Cove, or any quiet morning

Little River Road follows the river for miles with pull-offs every few hundred yards, so you can fish a run, drive two minutes, and fish another. Metcalf Bottoms is the wide, gentle stretch with the easiest wading in the park. The Sinks, a mile upstream, is the famous waterfall pool. People drown there most years; fish the runs above and below it, never the pool.

Best slot: Sunday afternoon on the drive in, if there is light left

Late November

How to catch one in cold water

Water is in the 40s. Trout stop chasing and sit deep in the slow water, eating what drifts to their nose. Three things matter more than anything: fish between about 11 and 3 when the water is warmest, get the lure down to the bottom, and move slowly.

SetupPark waterTown water
Fly rodTwo bead-head nymphs under a small indicator, size 16 to 18: Pheasant Tail, Hare's Ear, a small black stonefly. Short upstream casts, high rod, no slack. Forget dry flies this week.Same nymphs work. An egg pattern or a San Juan Worm is deadly on stocked fish.
Spinning rodSmall inline spinner, 1/16 oz, with the treble swapped for a single hook. Panther Martin or Rooster Tail. Cast up, retrieve slow and deep.The same, or just a size 10 single hook, a split shot, and a worm, a kernel of corn, or PowerBait on the bottom of a slow pool.
Line4 lb or 5X. The water is gin clear.4 to 6 lb.
Where in the streamDeep, slow pools and the soft water behind boulders. Not the fast riffles.Bridge pools and anywhere the current slows.

Safety, which in November means cold

Drowning is the second cause of death in the park and the water is numbing. Nobody wades above the knee. Felt or studded boots and a wading staff are not optional for anyone over sixty on these rocks. Fish from the bank wherever a pool can be reached from the bank. Layers, a dry change of clothes in the car, and a thermos. If someone goes in, the trip is over for the day; get them into the car with the heater on. The park dispatch number is 865-436-1294 and the nearest ER is LeConte in Sevierville, on the hub.

The stocking rhythm

Town fish go in on Thursday. By the following Wednesday most of them have been caught or have moved. So town water fishes best Friday and Saturday, worst Tuesday and Wednesday. This week Thursday is Thanksgiving and the town is closed to fishing anyway; the park is open and empty that morning if someone wants two hours before dinner.
