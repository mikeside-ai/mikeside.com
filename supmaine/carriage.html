✅ Before you ride

Tonight, in Union10 minutes

Sign all three waivers in the Bar Harbor Bicycle Shop portal — it's the difference between rolling at 9:10 and rolling at 9:35, and the whole day downstream is built on a 9:20 departure.

Charge every phone to 100%. Continuous GPS plus a screen is the fastest battery drain there is. One power bank in a jersey pocket or handlebar bag covers all three of you.

Pack two bottles of water each plus something salty. There is no water anywhere on the network until Jordan Pond House at post 15, roughly two-thirds of the way round.

At the counter, 9:00aask these four

1. Is "half day" a hard 1:00 return or a rolling four hours? That single answer decides whether the long loop is comfortable or a chase. Ask it before you sign anything.

2. Do you have locks? Bikes are banned from every hiking trail in the park, so the moment anyone wants to walk up to the Bubbles, you need to leave the bikes somewhere.

3. What's the realistic battery range in eco vs. turbo? 16.5 miles with 350 ft of climbing is nothing for a modern e-bike, but ask so nobody rides the first hour in turbo for fun.

4. Can we have a free carriage road map? They keep them behind the counter. Take three. They also live in a brochure box at the Eagle Lake Bridge entrance if you forget.

🧭 Turn by turn

This is the Tri-Lakes Loop — the park's own 10.9-mile circuit linking Eagle Lake, Bubble Pond and Jordan Pond — with 5.6 miles of road at each end to reach it from town. 16.5 miles total. The carriage roads have no street names: every junction is a numbered wooden post with arrows to named destinations, and the numbers are the only navigation that exists out there.

You ride it clockwise. Bridges crossed, in order: Eagle Lake (B), Bubble Pond (D), Triad–Day Mountain (M), Jordan Pond Dam (K), Deer Brook (E). NPS puts the loop at 1 hr 15 to 1 hr 45 on a manual bike — on e-bikes with stops, the schedule below has an hour of slack in it. The risk today is not distance. It is stopping too long in the first hour.

Leg by legtimes assume 9:20 rolling
9:20a0.0 mi

Out of town on ME-233

Left out of the shop onto Cottage St, then west on Eagle Lake Road (ME-233). 2.8 miles of pavement climbing about 350 feet. This is the only car traffic of the day and the shoulder is narrow in places — single file, and don't let the group string out. The motor makes the climb a non-event; ride it in eco and save the battery.
9:40a2.8 mi

Post 6 — Eagle Lake Bridge. Gravel starts.

Parking area on the right, brochure box, a non-flush toilet, and a wayside panel. Go under the stone arch and follow the signs for post 7 / Bubble Pond — south, down the east shore, with the lake on your right. Don't navigate by left and right here; navigate by the arrow that says 7. Riding this way round puts the flat half first and both climbs at the end, which is what you want with a fixed 1:00 return.
9:55a4.2 mi

6 → 7, the east shore of Eagle Lake

Flat, open water on your right, Cadillac and Pemetic rising straight out of the far shore. This is the prettiest and easiest mile and a half of the ride. Take the photos here, not later — the light on the lake is best before 10:30.
10:10a5.4 mi

7 → 17, past Bubble Pond

At post 7 keep heading south toward 17 (Wildwood Stables). You cross Bubble Pond Bridge and the road runs right along the waterline under Cadillac's west cliffs. Stop here for ten minutes. Most riders blow straight past and it's the quietest place on the loop. Do not swim — it's a public drinking-water supply and rangers enforce it.
10:40a7.4 mi

17 → 16 → 15

Over the Triad–Day Mountain Bridge. At 16 follow signs for Jordan Pond House. Post 15 is the house itself — bike racks out front, roughly two-thirds of the way round.
11:00a9.0 mi

Post 15 — Jordan Pond House. Turnaround.

Water, toilets, popovers. Do not join the sit-down wait list — August waits run 30 to 60 minutes and will eat the whole afternoon. Outdoor window only, and only if there's no line. Lock the bikes and walk five minutes to the north shore path for the Bubbles reflected in the water; the lawn view is the one everyone settles for and it's the worse frame. Back on the bikes by 11:25.
11:25a9.0 mi

15 → 14 → 10 — the climb

Over the Jordan Pond Dam Bridge, up the west side of the pond past Deer Brook Bridge. This is where the day gets earned: 10N/10S is a high point and the grade is steady. On an e-bike it's a non-event; if anyone's on a manual bike this is the hard part of their morning. Watch for the 10N/10S split — both are signed, take the one pointing to Eagle Lake / post 8.
11:55a11.5 mi

Post 8 — the high point, southwest corner of Eagle Lake

Second and last climb done. Everything from here is downhill or flat. Post 8 is also the junction where Around the Mountain and the Jordan Pond loop peel off — ignore both, they'd add hours you don't have. Follow signs for post 9 / Eagle Lake.
12:15p13.7 mi

8 → 9 → 6, back under the bridge

The west shore of Eagle Lake, heading north — fast and rolling. You come back under the Eagle Lake Bridge at post 6, having closed the loop.
12:30p13.7 mi

ME-233 downhill into town

2.8 miles, almost entirely coasting, roughly 12 minutes. It is a fast descent on a road with cars — this is the one stretch of the day where a spill would actually matter. Cover the brakes.
12:45p16.5 mi

Back at 141 Cottage St

Fifteen minutes to spare on a 1:00 return. Grab lunch in town as a bag, not a sit-down — eating it in the Jeep is what buys the 1:30 Beehive start and therefore the whole afternoon.

📷 Photo stops

In riding order. The lake light is best before 10:30 and the reflections need still water, so the morning half of this loop is the photographic half — which is lucky, because it's also the flat half.

9:40a · post 6

Under the Eagle Lake Bridge arch

Everyone shoots the bridge from the road above. Ride through the arch, stop, turn around and shoot back through it with the lake beyond — the stonework frames the water and you get a picture nobody else in the parking lot took.

9:55a · east shore, posts 6–7

Three bikes, no cars, receding gravel

This is the one that will actually mean something in ten years. Get 50 yards ahead, crouch low in the middle of the road, and shoot the other two riding at you with the tunnel of trees closing overhead. Burst mode, and let them get closer than feels right before you fire.

10:15a · Bubble Pond

Cadillac's west cliffs off the waterline

The road runs within a few feet of the water here with the cliffs rising straight out of the far side. Get low — phone almost at the surface — and you double the cliffs in the reflection. Morning is dead calm here far more often than Eagle Lake is.

11:05a · post 15, north shore

The Bubbles, reflected

The most photographed view in Maine and it earns it. Walk from the bike racks to the north shore path — five minutes, and it's the difference between the postcard and a snapshot of a lawn. Twin rounded peaks dead centre at the far end, mirror below. If there's a ripple, shoot it anyway; the view survives without the mirror.

11:35a · Deer Brook Bridge

Arches from below

One of the seventeen, on the climb up the west side of Jordan Pond. Scramble down to the brook and shoot back up through it. Deep shade, so brace the phone on a rock. Late-spring visitors report the brook running dry — in August after rain it should be flowing.

11:55a · post 8

Down onto Eagle Lake from the high point

Glimpses down through the trees to the lake with the mountains across it. It's the summit of your morning in every sense. Take the group shot here, at the point where the hard part is behind you and everyone's still pleased about it.

➕ Time allowing

Ranked by what's actually worth the minutes. Check the clock at post 8, not before. If you're through post 8 by noon you have room for one of these; if you're through by 11:40 you have room for the big one.

1. Add the Witch Hole Pond loop on the way homethe best add
Adds~4.7 mi
Time~40 min
Effortnearly flat
6→4→3→2→5→Duck Brook Rd

Instead of turning down ME-233 at post 6, follow signs north from 6 toward post 4. The connector runs past the Breakneck ponds and under the Eagle Lake Bridge, then loops Witch Hole Pond — beaver lodges, one pond full of standing dead trees, wild blueberries along the edges in August. It exits at Duck Brook Bridge near post 5, and Duck Brook Road drops you into town on a far gentler, quieter descent than ME-233.

Why this is the best add: it's flat, it replaces the one genuinely unpleasant stretch of the day, and it ends at Duck Brook Bridge — three granite arches, the showpiece of the seventeen, and the single best photograph on the network. Walk down to the streambed and shoot upstream; from the parapet on top you can't see the thing you came for.

Builds the day to ~21 mi · nps.gov Witch Hole Pond Loop ↗
2. Popovers at the outdoor window20 min, conditional

Only if there is no line at the walk-up window when you arrive at post 15. It's a genuinely good popover and it's the thing Jordan Pond is famous for. The instant it becomes a queue, it's twenty minutes of your afternoon for a bread roll — walk away.

3. Sit at Bubble Pond and do nothingunderrated

Twenty minutes on the rocks at the waterline. You are going to spend the afternoon on a summit road with several hundred other people and an iron-rung climb; this is the only genuinely quiet thing on Thursday's schedule. If the group is flagging, spend the time here rather than adding miles.

4. Around the Mountain from post 8the trap

Don't. It looks like a natural extension on the map and it's another 12 miles with the most sustained climbing on the network. It would put you back at the shop after 2:00, blow the bike return, and cost you the Beehive and the Cadillac window. Named here only so nobody proposes it at post 8 and it sounds reasonable.

5. Walk to the Bubbles from Jordan Pondout of reach today

South Bubble and Bubble Rock are a 40-minute detour from the Bubble Rock lot on Park Loop Road — but from the Jordan Pond House end, where the bikes put you, you walk a mile up the pond before you start climbing. That's 90 minutes, not 40. It doesn't fit a 1:00 bike return. Do it from the Jeep another year.

🚪 Bail-outs

Where you can shorten the ride, and — more usefully — where you can't.

At post 7, before Bubble Pond

Skip the southern half entirely: 7 → 8 → 9 → 6 is the Eagle Lake Loop on its own, 5.9 miles. Cuts about 5 miles and 45 minutes and still gives you the best water views. This is the decision point. If anyone is already unhappy at post 7, take it — the group's mood matters more than the extra lake.

Past post 17, there is no short way home

Once you're south of Bubble Pond, the only ways back are the way you came or over the climb to posts 10 and 8. There is no shortcut, no cut-through, and the free Island Explorer shuttle will not carry e-bikes. Committing at post 7 is committing. Fuel and water before you make that call.

If a battery dies

A dead e-bike is a very heavy normal bike — rideable, but the climb from 15 up to 10 and 8 becomes genuinely hard work. If someone's battery is under a third at post 15, put them in the lowest assist immediately and have the strongest rider take their bag. Call the shop at 207-288-3886 for the swap policy before you leave the counter, not after.

Fog is not a reason to bail

If the summit is socked in when you leave Union, the carriage roads are usually below the fog layer and completely unaffected. Eagle Lake in mist is better than Eagle Lake in sun. Check the marine layer, not the inland forecast.

📌 Rules that bite

⚡
Class 1 e-bikes only. Class 2 and 3 are banned from the carriage roads outright. 20 mph limit. The shop rents legal bikes — this matters if anyone suggests bringing their own another year.
🐎
Everyone yields to horses; cyclists yield to everyone. Slow to walking pace and give horses a wide berth — and do not ring a bell at a horse. Speak normally so the animal hears a human voice.
🏊
Eagle Lake, Bubble Pond and Jordan Pond are drinking-water supplies. Swimming and wading are prohibited and enforced. On a hot ride day this catches people out constantly. Echo Lake, on the west side, is the swimming lake.
🚫
Stay north of Jordan Pond House. The Land & Garden Preserve carriage roads around Little Long Pond, immediately south of post 15, ban bikes of every kind — e- or otherwise. Signed, but easy to roll past.
🥾
Bikes are banned from every hiking trail in the park. Including the Jordan Pond shore path. That's why you need locks to walk to the north shore.
🎫
Cyclists need the park entrance pass too and can be asked to show it on the carriage roads. Your QR is on the pass page — screenshot it before you leave Union.
📵
Assume no signal from post 6 onward. Screenshot the signpost chain, the Cadillac QR and the bike reservation tonight. The tracker's numbers work without signal; nothing else on this page will reload.
🐜
Ticks are active in August along the grassy carriage-road edges. Check at the Harraseeket tonight — deer ticks here are small enough to miss.
