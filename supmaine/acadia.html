Tonight, in Union — before you drive anywhere

✅ Cadillac slot is booked. Now get it off the internet.

There is no cell signal on the summit road and rangers scan at the gate. A reservation that only exists inside the recreation.gov app is a reservation you cannot show. Screenshot the QR tonight on wifi and put a copy on a second phone — one dead battery should not cost all three of you the summit.

You are holding two windows — 4:00p and 5:00p. You can only ever use one; the reservation is per vehicle and you have one Jeep. Pick one tonight and cancel the other on recreation.gov so it goes back in the pool for someone else's only day on the island. Which one, and why, is in the clock.

Whichever you keep, arrive inside it. Entry is checked at the gate on the way in only; once you are through you can stay as long as you like, including past sunset. Turning up late is a forfeited ticket, and there is no grace period.

Screenshot these too, same sitting: the park entrance pass QR (pass page — a separate thing from this reservation, and cyclists need it as well), the bike reservation, and the Harraseeket address.

The wind layer lives in the Jeep, not the hotel. The summit runs ten to fifteen degrees cooler than sea level and a great deal windier. Nobody wants to walk back down to the lot for a jacket.

If the day slips and you miss your window

Go late. The reservation covers daytime entry only — roughly 9:00a–4:30p — and outside that window the road is open to anyone with no ticket at all. A 4:45p drive-up needs no ticket and puts you on the summit in the best light of the day. Confirm the cutoff on recreation.gov before betting on it; the hours have shifted between seasons.
Walk up. The reservation is for vehicles. On foot the North Ridge Trail is open any time — 4.4 mi round trip, ~1,100 ft, open granite the whole way. That's a 2½-hour commitment, so it's a different day rather than a swap — but the summit is never actually closed to you.

The short version of my advice

Keep Option B — the bikes are booked and the carriage roads are the one thing Acadia has that no other park in the country does. Three amendments: Thunder Hole has to land between 3:15 and 4:15 (high tide is 5:05p — that's the only hour all week it actually booms), so build the afternoon around your booked summit time and that window; decide the ride length at the shop; and name the Beehive bail-out in the parking lot, not on the rungs.

🕒 The clock

Approximate times for the booked day — Option B, bikes at 9:00a. Everything before 1:00p is fixed by the bike return and everything after it is fixed by the tide and your summit window. Treat these as a spine, not a schedule: if you are 20 minutes down at post 8, you take the time out of Otter Cliff, not out of the Beehive.

Two summit windows booked. Keep the 5:00, release the 4:00.

The 5:00 is the better fit and it is not close. Three reasons. The afternoon currently has no slack anywhere — bike return at 1:00, an unknown 15 minutes in the Sand Beach lot, and a one-way committed climb you cannot hurry; the 5:00 gives you an hour of margin the 4:00 does not. Thunder Hole lands at 3:45 instead of 3:20, which is 80 minutes before the 5:05p high rather than 105 — materially more likely to actually boom, and that stop is worthless otherwise. And 5:00–6:00 is the good half of the summit's best light, with sunset not until 7:55.

The cost is real: you reach Freeport around 10:00p instead of 9:00p, after a 16.5-mile ride and a cliff climb. If that lands badly — or if anyone wants a proper sit-down dinner in Camden rather than a fast one — take the 4:00 and swap the Beehive for Gorham Mountain. What the summit time really decides is which mountain you climb.

🥾 Things to do

Ranked by what you'd regret missing. Nothing here takes more than two hours, which is the whole trick to a one-day island — pick three, not six. Turn-by-turn for all of it is in Routes.

Ride the carriage roads✅ bookedthe reason to come
Time3–4 hrs
Distance8 or 16.5 mi
Efforteasy on e-bike

Forty-five miles of crushed stone that Rockefeller built between 1913 and 1940, with seventeen stone bridges and not one car. Gravel is smooth enough for any bike; the motor turns the 350-ft climb out of town into nothing. Class 1 e-bikes only — Class 2 and 3 are banned — 20 mph limit, and you yield to walkers, horses and carriages. Don't ring a bell at a horse.

Good news on the numbers. The Thursday card calls the long ride "~21 mi." Measured against the park's own route lengths it's closer to 16.5 — the Tri-Lakes Loop is 10.9 miles and ME-233 adds 5.6 round trip. That's a meaningfully easier day than the card implies.

Stay north of Jordan Pond. The Land & Garden Preserve carriage roads around Little Long Pond ban bikes of every kind, e- or otherwise.

Climb the Beehiveiron rungs · exposed
Time1½–2 hrs
Stats1.6 mi · 508 ft
GradeHard

Iron rungs and ladders bolted across open cliff face four hundred feet above Sand Beach, with no guardrails. More psychological than physical — the moves are easy, the exposure is not. It is one-way up. No turning around, no passing, no talking someone down once they've started.

Have the conversation in the parking lot. Anyone even slightly unsure takes Gorham Mountain instead. And the answer changes entirely if the granite is wet — do not attempt it in rain or within a couple of hours of it.

Not a peregrine closure. The 2026 closures are Precipice, Jordan Cliffs and Valley Cove only, whatever half the hiking blogs say. Confirm same-day at nps.gov/acad conditions ↗. Dogs not allowed.

🧭 Routes, step by step

The carriage roads have no street names and no GPS. Every junction has a wooden signpost with a number on it and arrows to named destinations, and those numbers match the free map. You navigate by post number and nothing else — which is why the paper map is not optional. Sequences and distances below are the park's own.

The ride has its own page now

Carriage roads — turn by turn ↗ takes the Tri-Lakes sequence below and expands it into leg-by-leg directions with clock times, photo stops in riding order, ranked add-ons if you're running early, and the bail-out points. It also carries a live GPS tracker — distance and bearing to ten verified landmarks, which keeps working with no cell signal. Open it tonight to grant location permission and cache the map tiles; both need wifi.

Get the map before you ride

Free carriage road maps live in a brochure box at the carriage road entrance, at Hulls Cove Visitor Center, and behind the counter at most bike shops — ask for one when you pick up the bikes. There is no signal out there, so also screenshot the route sequences below. Official maps and elevation profiles: nps.gov carriage roads ↗

● high point · ● Jordan Pond House
  1. Out of town on ME-233 west — 2.8 mi, paved, ~350 ft of climb, the only traffic of the day. Gravel starts at the Eagle Lake Bridge parking area. This is post 6.
  2. At 6, head south down the east shore of Eagle Lake toward post 7. This is the flat, scenic half, with Cadillac and Pemetic rising straight out of the far shore. Best water views of the ride.
  3. At 7, continue south to 17 (Wildwood Stables), passing Bubble Pond on the way — the carriage road runs right along the waterline under Cadillac's west cliffs. Ten minutes here is the quietest stop on the loop and most riders blow straight past it.
  4. 17 → 16 → 15. Post 15 is the Jordan Pond House — bike racks out front, roughly two-thirds of the way round. Turnaround, stretch, popovers from the outdoor window only if there's no line. Walk to the north shore for the Bubbles reflected in the water.
  5. 15 → 14 → 10. Here's where the day gets earned: 10N/10S is a high point and the climb starts. On an e-bike it's a non-event; on a manual bike it's the hard part.
  6. 10 → 8 — the second high point, on the southwest corner of Eagle Lake. From 8 it's downhill.
  7. 8 → 9 → 6, back under the Eagle Lake Bridge, then ME-233 downhill into town. The road leg home is fast and mostly coasting.

The Beehive — step by stepone-way up
  1. Park at Sand Beach and cross Park Loop Road carefully. The trailhead sign is on the west side.
  2. Start on the Bowl Trail — easy woods walking for about 0.2 mi.
  3. Beehive Trail splits right. This is the decision point and the last one you get. Everything above here is committed: iron rungs, ladders and narrow ledges across open cliff, roughly 500 feet of climbing with the drop under your right shoulder.
  4. Summit — the whole crescent of Sand Beach and Newport Cove below you, open Atlantic behind. Sit down, drink something. This is the payoff.
  5. Descend north-west to The Bowl, a small mountain pond in a granite basin. The descent is a normal trail — no rungs.
  6. Bowl Trail back down to where you started. Loop total 1.4–1.6 mi, 1½–2 hrs unhurried with photo stops.
Gorham Mountain — step by stepno exposure
  1. Gorham Mountain parking lot on Park Loop Road, south of Thunder Hole just past Monument Cove. Its own lot, usually easier than Sand Beach.
  2. Climb the ridge — steady, open granite, cairns marking the way, ocean on your right almost continuously.
  3. Optional near the bottom: the Cadillac Cliffs Trail branches off and rejoins higher up — it threads under an overhanging ancient sea cliff. Slower and rockier, worth it if you're not chasing a clock.
  4. Summit at 525 ft — and this is the view of the Beehive, with Sand Beach beyond it. Arguably the better photograph of the two hikes.
  5. Back the way you came. 1.8 mi round trip, about an hour. Or continue north to The Bowl and down the Bowl Trail to link up with anyone who took the rungs.
Ocean Path — step by stepflat · turn back anywhere
  1. Start at the far end of the Sand Beach lot, above the beach steps.
  2. 0.6 mi to Thunder Hole — wide gravel path along the cliff edge, never out of sight of the water.
  3. 0.2 mi further to Monument Cove, a pocket beach of round cobbles with a granite spire standing offshore. Most people walk past it; look down and to your right.
  4. 1.4 mi to Otter Cliff — 110 feet of sheer pink granite. Rock climbers are usually roped up on it in summer.
  5. 2 mi to Otter Point and the end of the path. Turn around whenever you like — this is the rare Acadia walk with no commitment at all.
Ship Harbor — step by steptime it to the 10:48a low
  1. Ship Harbor lot on ME-102A, a couple of miles east of Bass Harbor Head Light.
  2. Take the figure-eight — signed, flat, gravel, stroller-friendly. Either lobe works; the outer one reaches the shore sooner.
  3. Out to the open granite ledge at the harbor mouth. This is the tidepool ground — sea stars, green crabs, urchins, anemones in the deeper pools.
  4. Step on bare rock, never rockweed. Wet seaweed on sloping granite is the single most common injury on this coast.
  5. Loop back through the spruce. 1.3–1.5 mi, 30 minutes, and Wonderland is a mile up the road if you want the better ledge too.

🗻 Places to see

Short stops — park, look, go. The east side of the island is the famous half; the quiet side is where you go to be alone. You cannot do both well in one day, and pretending otherwise is how people end up spending Thursday in a Jeep.

Cadillac Mountain✅ slot booked
Stop length45–60 min
Summit loop0.4 mi paved
Your window4:00 or 5:00p

1,530 feet, the highest point on the North Atlantic seaboard, and you drive to the top of it. The paved summit loop is wheelchair-accessible and takes eleven minutes; the good part is walking fifty yards off it onto open granite and sitting down. Frenchman Bay, the Porcupine Islands, and on a clear day Katahdin 120 miles north.

Midday is the weakest hour up there — flat light, haze, full lot. Late afternoon has softer light on the bay and half the crowd. If your window is an early one, budget the full hour and walk further off the loop — the crowd thins with every fifty yards.

Ten to fifteen degrees cooler than sea level and much windier. The wind layer lives in the Jeep, not the hotel.

Jordan Pond & the Bubblespost 15
Stop length20 min

Twin rounded peaks reflected in water so clear you can see forty feet down — the most photographed view in Maine and it earns it. Stand on the north shore, not the lawn, for the clean reflection. The reflection needs still water, so a breezy afternoon gives you the view without the mirror.

Do not join the restaurant wait list. August sit-down waits run 30–60 minutes and will eat your entire return buffer. Popovers from the outdoor window only if there's no line.

A narrow granite chamber that traps air under an incoming wave and claps like a cannon, throwing spray over the railing. It only performs one to two hours before high water with a swell running. Acadia's only lighthouse, bolted to a granite headland since 1858. Take the stairway down the cliff face to the rocks — the view from the parking lot is nothing, the view from below is the postcard.

📷 Photo ops

One rule organizes all of this: the east side of the island wants morning light, the quiet side wants afternoon. Option B does the east side in the afternoon, which means the cliffs sit in shadow — not a disaster, but shoot with the sun behind you looking south along the coast rather than north into it, and the water goes deep blue instead of blown out.

3:45p · the money shot of the day

Thunder Hole, mid-flood

Ninety minutes before the 5:05p high with a swell running is when the chamber actually claps. Shoot from the upper platform, not the lower one — you'll stay dry and you get the whole plume against the granite. Burst mode; the spray peaks for under a second. If it's running hard, the lower railing soaks people and the rock gets slick.

5:00–6:00p · Cadillac summit

Frenchman Bay and the Porcupines

Walk the summit loop to the north-facing ledges and look down on the Porcupine Islands scattered across the bay with Bar Harbor tucked in at the left. Late afternoon side-light picks out the islands; at noon they flatten into haze. A person standing on the bare pink granite gives it scale — without one it reads as a postcard of nothing.

11:00a · Jordan Pond, post 15

The Bubbles, reflected

Get to the north shore path, not the lawn by the restaurant — the classic frame puts the two rounded peaks dead center at the far end with the mirror below them. It only mirrors when the water is still, so it's a morning subject; by late morning in August there's usually a ripple. Shoot it anyway and take the reflection as a bonus.

2:45–3:15p · from the Beehive summit

Sand Beach from above

The single best frame on the east side: the whole crescent of Sand Beach and Newport Cove four hundred feet below with the open Atlantic behind it. Afternoon puts the sun to the southwest, so you're shooting slightly into it — expose for the water, let the granite go dark, and it works. Same view without the rungs from the Gorham Mountain ridge, which also gets the Beehive itself in the frame.

9:25a · Duck Brook, post 5

Three arches from the streambed

Scramble down to the brook on the downstream side and shoot back up through the arches. Deep shade at that hour, so it's a slow exposure — brace the phone on a rock. This is the shot almost nobody who visits Acadia comes home with, because almost nobody rides the short loop.

9:55a · the carriage roads themselves

Three bikes, no cars, receding gravel

The one that will actually mean something in ten years. Get ahead, crouch low in the middle of the road, and shoot the other two riding toward you with the tunnel of trees closing overhead. The east shore of Eagle Lake between posts 7 and 9 is the prettiest stretch for it.

late afternoon only

Bass Harbor Head Light from the rocks below

Down the cliff stairway, then right onto the granite. The lighthouse faces southwest, so this is a 4:00p-to-sunset subject and a waste of a morning — which is worth knowing before Option C schedules it at 9:30a. At sunset expect twenty photographers on the same slab, and expect to have arrived an hour early to get a spot.

🦞 Eat

The rule for this day: the sightseeing sets the clock and food fits around it. You're on an island covered in places to eat — the mistake is spending ninety minutes of daylight driving to one specific pound.

Bar Harbor Lobster Poundopens 11:00 · fastest

414 ME-3 by the Hulls Cove entrance — ten minutes from the east-side stops instead of forty-five. Order at the window, line moves, 11:00 is the zero-wait slot. Rolls $30–33. This is the answer that costs you twenty minutes of daylight instead of ninety.

Thurston's, Bernardonly on C, D or E

Awning-covered decks over Bass Harbor with boats unloading the catch you're about to eat. It is the better room, no argument — but from the east side it's 45 minutes each way plus a 75-minute sit. Take it only if the day already puts you on the quiet side. Some seasons they open at noon with a queue already formed; be there 11:45 or slide to 12:30.

On Option B: lunch is a bag1:00p, not a stop

Grab something in town on the walk back from the bike shop and eat it in the Jeep. That single decision is what buys the 1:45 Beehive start — and therefore the whole afternoon. Two bottles of water each, minimum, plus something salty: there is no water anywhere on the carriage roads until Jordan Pond House at post 15.

Dinner — don't book it~7:45p on the 5:00

Camden or Rockland is the natural break on the drive south — around 7:45p if you keep the 5:00 summit, closer to 6:45p on the 4:00. Young's in Belfast is on the route but posted hours conflict badly across sources and one recent listing closes them at 3pm — call 207-338-1160 if it matters. Otherwise the Harraseeket's own Broad Arrow Tavern runs late and is downstairs from your bed, which on a 10:00p arrival is the realistic answer.

Grab the free carriage road map at the bike shop — the numbered signposts are the only navigation out there and there's no signal to fall back on. Screenshot the route sequences above too.
🎫
Park entrance pass — $35/vehicle, seven days, separate from the Cadillac reservation. Your QR is on the pass page. Cyclists need it too and can be asked to show it on the carriage roads.
🅿️
Sand Beach parking, honestly: that lot runs full from about 9:30a to 3:00p in August and turns over one car at a time. The 1:20p arrival on the clock above is the tight moment of the afternoon. Roadside in the marked right lane of Park Loop past the beach is legal and normal; Gorham Mountain lot is the backup. Budget fifteen minutes, not zero.
📵
Cell service is unreliable park-wide and absent on the summit road. Screenshot the Cadillac QR, the bike reservation and the Harraseeket address before you leave Union. Download offline maps for MDI tonight.
👟
Pack hiking shoes where you can reach them in the Jeep, not buried. You're changing in a parking lot with a reservation clock running.
🚲
Ask the bike shop two things: is "half day" a hard 1:00 return or a rolling four hours — that answer alone decides whether the Beehive is comfortable or rushed — and do you have locks, because bikes are banned from every hiking trail. Sign all three waivers in the customer portal tonight.
🌫️
Check the fog before you leave Union, not the inland forecast. If the summit is socked in, the carriage roads are usually below the fog layer and completely unaffected — Eagle Lake in mist is better than Eagle Lake in sun.
🦅
2026 peregrine closures: Precipice, Jordan Cliffs, Valley Cove. The Beehive is not among them. Confirm same-day at nps.gov/acad conditions ↗.
🐜
Ticks are active in August along grassy carriage-road edges and the Bowl Trail. Check at the hotel — deer ticks here are small enough to miss.
🚌
The Island Explorer shuttle is free and stops at Sand Beach, Jordan Pond and the visitor center. Worth knowing if a lot is a circus — but it won't carry e-bikes.
