Do this first — tomorrow morning

Cadillac timed entry drops Tue Aug 4, 10:00 AM ET

Seventy percent of Thursday's summit-road slots release two days out — 10:00 AM ET tomorrow, the exact minute you're boarding DL0426 at Detroit. It is the only piece of Thursday that can be permanently lost, and it costs $6.

The plan: 9:50a alarm. All three of you on recreation.gov at once from the gate, logged in with payment saved before 9:55. First one through wins, the others stop. Screenshot the QR — no cell signal on the summit road and rangers scan at the gate.

Check tonight too: 30% released back in early May and there are often leftovers. A slot today beats a scramble tomorrow.

Two real fallbacks if it sells out

Go late. The reservation covers daytime entry only — roughly 9:00a–4:30p — and outside that window the road is open to anyone. A 4:45p drive-up needs no ticket and puts you on the summit in the best light of the day. Confirm the cutoff on recreation.gov before betting on it; the hours have shifted between seasons.
Walk up. The reservation is for vehicles. On foot the North Ridge Trail is open any time — 4.4 mi round trip, ~1,100 ft, open granite the whole way. That's a 2½-hour commitment, so it's a different day rather than a swap — but the summit is never actually closed to you.

The short version of my advice

Keep Option B — the bikes are booked and the carriage roads are the one thing Acadia has that no other park in the country does. Three amendments: book the 4:00 summit window instead of 2:00 and put Thunder Hole in front of it at 3:30 (high tide is 5:05p — that's the only hour all week it actually booms); decide the ride length at the shop, because riding 21 miles and assuming the afternoon still holds is the one combination that doesn't work; and name the Beehive bail-out in the parking lot, not on the rungs. Detail on all three below.

🥾 Things to do

Ranked by what you'd regret missing. Nothing here takes more than two hours, which is the whole trick to a one-day island — pick three, not six.

Ride the carriage roads✅ bookedthe reason to come
Time3–4 hrs
Distance13 or 21 mi
Efforteasy on e-bike

Forty-five miles of crushed stone that Rockefeller built by hand between 1913 and 1940, with seventeen stone bridges and not one car. Gravel is smooth enough for any bike; the motor turns the 350-ft climb out of town into nothing. Class 1 e-bikes only — Class 2 and 3 are banned — 20 mph limit, and you yield to walkers, horses and carriages. Don't ring a bell at a horse.

The two versions, and this is the real decision of the day:
• Long, ~21 mi — Eagle Lake → Bubble Pond → Jordan Pond and back. The scenic heart of the system. Returns you at 12:45 with zero slack and tired legs, 45 minutes before iron rungs.
• Short, ~13 mi — Duck Brook Bridge → Witch Hole Pond loop. Nearly flat, a mile from the shop, beaver lodges and wild blueberries in August, and Duck Brook is the most beautiful built thing in the park. Banks a full hour for the afternoon.

Stay north of Jordan Pond. The Land & Garden Preserve carriage roads around Little Long Pond ban bikes of every kind, e- or otherwise.

In: B only · 9:00a pickup, 1:00p return · Bar Harbor Bicycle Shop, 141 Cottage St
Climb the Beehiveiron rungs · exposed
Time1½–2 hrs
Stats1.6 mi · 508 ft
GradeHard

Iron rungs and ladders bolted across open cliff face four hundred feet above Sand Beach, with no guardrails. More psychological than physical — the moves are easy, the exposure is not. It is one-way up. No turning around, no passing, no talking someone down once they've started, and I've watched that go badly more than once.

Have the conversation in the parking lot. Anyone even slightly unsure takes Gorham Mountain instead. And the answer changes entirely if the granite is wet — do not attempt it in rain or within a couple of hours of it.

Not a peregrine closure. The 2026 closures are Precipice, Jordan Cliffs and Valley Cove only, whatever half the hiking blogs say. Confirm same-day at nps.gov/acad conditions ↗. Dogs not allowed.

In: B · 1:30p · park at Sand Beach, trailhead via the Bowl Trail
Gorham Mountainthe Beehive swap
Time~1 hr
Stats1.8 mi · 525 ft
GradeModerate

Open granite ridge with the ocean on your right the entire way up and no exposure at all. Same stretch of Park Loop, own parking lot, and honestly the better view of the two — you're looking at the Beehive and Sand Beach rather than standing on top of them. This is not a consolation prize, and if anyone in the group is on the fence about rungs, taking Gorham as a group beats splitting up.

Walk the Ocean Patheasy · flat
Time30 min–2 hrs
Stats0.6–2 mi each way
GradeEasy

The most spectacular easy mile on the Eastern seaboard, running along the cliff edge from Sand Beach past Thunder Hole to Otter Point. Flat, wide, and it never leaves the water. Do the 0.6 mi to Thunder Hole if you're short, or push on to Otter Cliff — 110 feet of sheer pink granite straight out of the Atlantic, the tallest headland on the US coast north of Rio.

In: A, D, E · morning · in B it becomes the 3:30 Thunder Hole stop
South Bubble & Bubble Rockbest payoff per minute
Time~40 min
Stats1.2 mi · 295 ft
GradeModerate

A glacial erratic the size of a car balanced on the lip of a cliff, carried here from forty miles away and dropped when the ice melted. Everyone takes the same photo of someone pretending to push it off, and everyone should. The view down the length of Jordan Pond from up there is the best in the park.

On the bike day: lock up at the junction and walk it — bikes are banned on every hiking trail in the park, which is exactly why you ask the shop for locks.

Ship Harbor and Wonderland, two flat loops a mile apart that both end on open granite shore. Thursday's 10:48a low is close to ideal timing — sea stars, green crabs, urchins, anemones in the pools. 🗻 Places to see

Short stops — park, look, go. The east side of the island is the famous half; the quiet side is where you go to be alone. You cannot do both well in one day, and pretending otherwise is how people end up spending Thursday in a Jeep.

Cadillac Mountain⚠ reservation required
Stop length45–60 min
Summit loop0.4 mi paved
Best light4:00–6:00p

1,530 feet, the highest point on the North Atlantic seaboard, and you drive to the top of it. The paved summit loop is wheelchair-accessible and takes eleven minutes; the good part is walking fifty yards off it onto open granite and sitting down. Frenchman Bay, the Porcupine Islands, and on a clear day Katahdin 120 miles north.

Midday is the weakest hour up there — flat light, haze, full lot. Late afternoon has softer light on the bay and half the crowd, which is the real argument for the 4:00 window over the 2:00.

Ten to fifteen degrees cooler than sea level and much windier. The wind layer lives in the Jeep, not the hotel.

In: every option · B at 3:45–4:30 · E at 9:00a
Jordan Pond & the Bubbles20 min
Stop length20 min
Full loop3.3 mi flat
Best lightcalm mornings

Twin rounded peaks reflected in water so clear you can see forty feet down — the most photographed view in Maine and it earns it. Stand on the north shore, not the lawn, for the clean reflection. The reflection needs still water, so a breezy afternoon gives you the view without the mirror.

Do not join the restaurant wait list. August sit-down waits run 30–60 minutes and will eat your entire return buffer. Popovers from the outdoor window only if there's no line.

In: A, B (bike turnaround 11:20a) · dropped in D
Thunder Hole3:15–4:15p only
Stop length15 min
High tide5:05p
Best window3:15–4:15p

A narrow granite chamber that traps air under an incoming wave and claps like a cannon, throwing spray over the railing. It only performs one to two hours before high water with a swell running. Thursday that is 3:15 to 4:15 in the afternoon — and it sits directly on the one-way Park Loop between Sand Beach and the summit road, so on Option B you drive past it anyway.

At any other hour, including the 9:45a slot on the current Option A card, it is a scenic crack in the rocks. Manage expectations or move the stop.

Slot it at 3:30 in B · in A/D/E it lands at the wrong tide
Bass Harbor Head Lightafternoon light only
Stop length30 min
Walk0.4 mi · 62 ft
Best lightlate afternoon

Acadia's only lighthouse, bolted to a granite headland since 1858. Take the stairway down the cliff face to the rocks — the view from the parking lot is nothing, the view from below is the postcard.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: it faces southwest. The morning slot on Option C at 9:30a is the worst light of the day — flat and backlit. This is an afternoon or sunset subject. The tiny lot is the trade-off: calm at 9:30, gridlocked at sunset. No buses, trailers or RVs; the road is too narrow.

In: C, D, E

📷 Photo ops

One rule organizes all of this: the east side of the island wants morning light, the quiet side wants afternoon. Option B does the east side in the afternoon, which means the cliffs sit in shadow — not a disaster, but shoot with the sun behind you looking south along the coast rather than north into it, and the water goes deep blue instead of blown out.

3:30p · the money shot of the day

Thunder Hole, mid-flood

Ninety minutes before the 5:05p high with a swell running is when the chamber actually claps. Shoot from the upper platform, not the lower one — you'll stay dry and you get the whole plume against the granite. Burst mode; the spray peaks for under a second. If it's running hard, the lower railing soaks people and the rock gets slick.

4:15–4:45p · Cadillac summit

Frenchman Bay and the Porcupines

Walk the summit loop counterclockwise to the north-facing ledges and look down on the Porcupine Islands scattered across the bay with Bar Harbor tucked in at the left. Late afternoon side-light picks out the islands; at noon they flatten into haze. A person standing on the bare pink granite gives it scale — without one it reads as a postcard of nothing.

11:20a · Jordan Pond north shore

The Bubbles, reflected

Get to the north shore path, not the lawn by the restaurant — the classic frame puts the two rounded peaks dead center at the far end with the mirror below them. It only mirrors when the water is still, so it's a morning subject; by late morning in August there's usually a ripple. Shoot it anyway and take the reflection as a bonus.

2:30–3:15p · from the Beehive summit

Sand Beach from above

The single best frame on the east side: the whole crescent of Sand Beach and Newport Cove four hundred feet below with the open Atlantic behind it. Afternoon puts the sun to the southwest, so you're shooting slightly into it — expose for the water, let the granite go dark, and it works. Same view without the rungs from the Gorham Mountain ridge, which also gets the Beehive itself in the frame.

9:25a · Duck Brook

Three arches from the streambed

Scramble down to the brook on the downstream side and shoot back up through the arches. Deep shade at that hour, so it's a slow exposure — brace the phone on a rock. This is the shot almost nobody who visits Acadia comes home with, because almost nobody rides the short loop.

any time · the carriage roads themselves

Three bikes, no cars, receding gravel

The one that will actually mean something in ten years. Get ahead, crouch low in the middle of the road, and shoot the other two riding toward you with the tunnel of trees closing overhead. Works anywhere on the Eagle Lake or Witch Hole loops.

late afternoon only

Bass Harbor Head Light from the rocks below

Down the cliff stairway, then right onto the granite. The lighthouse faces southwest, so this is a 4:00p-to-sunset subject and a waste of a morning — which is worth knowing before Option C schedules it at 9:30a. At sunset expect twenty photographers on the same slab, and expect to have arrived an hour early to get a spot.

🦞 Eat

The rule for this day: the sightseeing sets the clock and food fits around it. You're on an island covered in places to eat — the mistake is spending ninety minutes of daylight driving to one specific pound.

Bar Harbor Lobster Poundopens 11:00 · fastest

414 ME-3 by the Hulls Cove entrance — ten minutes from the east-side stops instead of forty-five. Order at the window, line moves, 11:00 is the zero-wait slot. Rolls $30–33. This is the answer that costs you twenty minutes of daylight instead of ninety.

Thurston's, Bernardonly on C, D or E

Awning-covered decks over Bass Harbor with boats unloading the catch you're about to eat. It is the better room, no argument — but from the east side it's 45 minutes each way plus a 75-minute sit. Take it only if the day already puts you on the quiet side. Some seasons they open at noon with a queue already formed; be there 11:45 or slide to 12:30.

On Option B: lunch is a bagnot a stop

Grab something in town on the walk back from the bike shop and eat it in the Jeep. That single decision is what buys the 1:30 Beehive start instead of 2:00 — and therefore the whole afternoon. Two bottles of water each, minimum, plus something salty: there is no water anywhere on the carriage roads until Jordan Pond House.

Dinner — don't book it

Camden or Rockland around 6:30–7:00 is the natural break on the drive south. Young's in Belfast is on the route but posted hours conflict badly across sources and one recent listing closes them at 3pm — call 207-338-1160 if it matters. Otherwise the Harraseeket's own Broad Arrow Tavern runs late and is downstairs from your bed.

📌 Field notes

🎫
Park entrance pass — $35/vehicle, seven days, separate from the Cadillac reservation. Your QR is on the pass page. Checked at the Sand Beach entrance station.
🅿️
Sand Beach parking, honestly: that lot runs full from about 9:30a to 3:00p in August and turns over one car at a time. The current card's claim that 1:25p is easy is optimistic. Roadside in the marked right lane of Park Loop past the beach is legal and normal; Gorham Mountain lot is the backup. Budget fifteen minutes, not zero.
📵
Cell service is unreliable park-wide and absent on the summit road. Screenshot the Cadillac QR, the bike reservation and the Harraseeket address before you leave Union. Download offline maps for MDI tonight.
👟
Pack hiking shoes where you can reach them in the Jeep, not buried. You're changing in a parking lot with a reservation clock running.
🚲
Ask the bike shop two things: is "half day" a hard 1:00 return or a rolling four hours — that answer alone decides whether the Beehive is comfortable or rushed — and do you have locks, because bikes are banned from every hiking trail. Sign all three waivers in the customer portal tonight.
🌫️
Check the fog before you leave Union, not the inland forecast. If the summit is socked in, the carriage roads are usually below the fog layer and completely unaffected — Eagle Lake in mist is better than Eagle Lake in sun.
🦅
2026 peregrine closures: Precipice, Jordan Cliffs, Valley Cove. The Beehive is not among them. Confirm same-day at nps.gov/acad conditions ↗.
🪳
Ticks are active in August along grassy carriage-road edges and the Bowl Trail. Check at the hotel — deer ticks here are small enough to miss.
🚌
The Island Explorer shuttle is free and stops at Sand Beach, Jordan Pond and the visitor center. Worth knowing if a lot is a circus — but it won't carry e-bikes.
